Picture a Calgary driver who has done three winters in an FWD Civic on all-seasons and gives up after a chinook freeze-thaw leaves half the commute under packed slush. Say they pay $24,500 for a 2018 Outback. The Outback handles the slush better, as you'd expect. The part people underestimate is resale: a year or so later, a clean 2018 Outback could still sell for close to what they paid. Out West, used Outbacks stop losing much value after a certain age, which makes them cheap to own, and the people who buy them know it.

This review looks at the 2016-2022 Outback as a used buy in 2026. There are plenty of new-car reviews. What a used buyer needs is how these cars do in real Canadian winters and what goes wrong with them: head gaskets, the CVT, oil consumption.

## How does Subaru's symmetrical AWD perform in Canadian winters?

Subaru has sold four-wheel-drive cars since the 1970s, and its [Symmetrical](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) full-time AWD is the result. An on-demand system waits for a sensor to report slip before it drives the rear axle. The Outback's lengthwise boxer engine and drivetrain feed all four wheels all the time, so in wet snow there's no lag to wait out. You still need winter tires. With Blizzaks on, an Outback handles more kinds of bad road than anything else at its price.

Compared with the reactive AWD in a RAV4, CR-V, Rogue or CX-5, the Subaru is better, but only in certain conditions. In typical Tier-1 metropolitan winter commuting on plowed roads, the difference is small enough that proper winter tires matter more than the AWD architecture. In rural Quebec, Northern Ontario, or BC interior driving where road grip varies unpredictably across the four wheels and snow accumulation can exceed 8 inches, the Subaru advantage is genuine and measurable. Owners say the system seems to "know what it is going to do before you do". That's close to the truth, since the torque is already spread across the wheels when a slip starts.

If you commute in Toronto, Vancouver or Calgary, choosing between an Outback, a CX-5 and a RAV4 is really about the cabin, fuel economy and cargo space. If you live rurally somewhere winter runs the show, the Outback's always-on AWD is worth paying for.

## Which Outback model years and trims offer the best used value?

Both the 2015-2019 fifth generation and the 2020-2024 sixth generation have a few standout buys in 2026. Our favourite is the 2019 2.5i Touring: the last and most sorted year of the fifth generation, well equipped, and priced below the redesign. A 2019 Touring with 90,000 to 130,000 km in Vancouver or Calgary lists between $24,000 and $28,000. That's well under a 2020-2022 car, and it does nearly everything the newer one does.

The sixth generation had teething problems in its 2020 launch year. Subaru issued service bulletins for infotainment glitches and certain shift-quality concerns that were addressed through software updates. Buy a 2021 or later and you avoid them. The 2.5i Touring or Limited is right for most people. The 2.4L turbo XT adds $3,000 to $5,000 and mostly makes sense if you tow. The Wilderness (2022+) is built for gravel, with raised suspension and Yokohama Geolandar all-terrain tires that grip well off-pavement and drone on the highway.

On a smaller budget, look at a 2017 or 2018 Touring at $19,000 to $22,000 with 130,000 to 170,000 km. By those years the fifth-generation 2.5L FB25 had shed its early quirks, and it's a durable engine.

## What are the known reliability issues on 2016-2022 Outbacks?

Three problems come up again and again in Canadian Outback service records, and your PPI should look for each. First, oil consumption on early FB-series engines. The US class action covered the 2013 Outback (the first year with the FB25), plus the 2011-2014 Forester and 2013 Legacy, and Subaru extended warranty coverage on affected cars. If you're looking at an older Outback, check the service history to see whether it qualified and got the fix. The 2016-onward cars this review covers are generally clear.

Second, the Lineartronic [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) on pre-2019 Outbacks is less consistent than the improved post-2019 unit. Subaru's CVT is not affected by the same JATCO durability issues that plagued 2013-2018 Nissan applications, but fluid maintenance is more critical than on a conventional automatic. Verify CVT fluid change history at the manufacturer-specified interval (typically 60,000 to 100,000 km) before purchasing any Outback past 100,000 km. Third, head gaskets on the older EJ-series boxers, though that's only a problem on Outbacks before the 2013 model year. The FB-series boxer engines fitted to 2013 and newer Outbacks are not subject to the head gasket failure mode.

On 2020+ Outbacks, know about one service bulletin: the EyeSight stereo cameras at the windshield require recalibration after any windshield replacement, so if a used Outback has had glass work, ask for proof of recalibration. Without recalibration, the [EyeSight](/glossary/eyesight/) driver assistance features may operate inaccurately.

## How does the Outback compare to a Mazda CX-5 or Toyota RAV4 in snow?

For absolute deep-snow capability and continuous variable-grip conditions, the Outback wins. Always-on AWD plus 8.7 inches of ground clearance (the CX-5 has 7.6, the RAV4 8.4) is a combination no reactive-AWD rival matches. The boxer engine's low centre of gravity helps cornering stability in slippery conditions.

On plowed city roads the gap mostly closes. A CX-5 with [i-Activ AWD](/glossary/awd/) and quality winter tires performs nearly identically to an Outback with the same tires in Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, Edmonton, Montreal, or Ottawa daily commuting conditions. The CX-5 has a nicer cabin and uses less fuel. The RAV4 resells better and offers more hybrid choice.

So it depends on where you live and how often you drive in the bad stuff. A city commuter in Vancouver, Toronto or Montreal who heads to the hills some weekends can pick any of the three, as long as it's on winter tires. In rural Quebec, Northern Ontario or the BC interior, buy the Outback and accept the plainer cabin.

## What does a used Outback cost in Toronto, Vancouver, and Montreal in 2026?

Prices depend on city and trim. A 2018 2.5i Touring with 110,000 to 140,000 km lists at approximately: Toronto $22,000-$26,000, Vancouver $23,000-$27,500, Montreal $20,000-$24,500, Calgary $22,000-$26,500, Edmonton $21,000-$25,500, Ottawa $21,500-$25,000. Vancouver costs more because west-coast buyers like the Outback as a near-luxury wagon. Montreal is a little cheaper because the Mazda CX-5 GT competes harder in Quebec.

For 2020-2022 sixth-generation Touring trims, expect to pay roughly $4,000 to $7,000 more than the equivalent fifth-generation. The 2.4L XT turbo adds another $3,000 to $5,000 over the 2.5i. The 2022+ Wilderness trim commands a premium of $4,000 to $6,000 over the equivalent Touring of the same year. Cargo-focused buyers may find the Outback less competitive than a CR-V Hybrid, RAV4 Hybrid, or CX-50 in 2026 pricing on usable cargo capacity per dollar.

## How well does the Outback resist rust on Canadian roads?

A 2016+ Outback copes reasonably well with salt, but it does rust. The usual spots are the rear subframe mounts, where salt collects, the rocker panel undercoating on cars that rarely got a winter wash, and exhaust hangers, which rot from below. The frame and unibody sheet metal are well-galvanized on modern production and rarely show structural rust.

For any used Outback that has spent winters in Quebec, Atlantic Canada, Northern Ontario, or rural Saskatchewan and Manitoba, make sure the PPI includes a careful look underneath. Vehicles with documented winter undercoating service typically show much better long-term corrosion resistance than vehicles that received no protection. Subaru owners in BC and Alberta tend to keep up with undercoating, which is one reason a western Outback often looks better at 200,000+ km than an eastern one.

## What maintenance does an Outback need before 250,000 km?

A modern 2016+ Outback with regular maintenance reaches 200,000 to 250,000 km without major repair work. What matters: oil changes at 8,000 to 10,000 km intervals with the manufacturer-specified grade; CVT fluid change at the manufacturer-specified interval (60,000 to 100,000 km depending on model year); spark plug replacement at the manufacturer interval (typically 100,000 to 160,000 km); brake pad and rotor replacement when wear indicators trigger (usually 80,000 to 120,000 km on front, 100,000 to 150,000 km on rear). The 2.5L FB25 is interference-engine timing chain (not belt), so no scheduled timing-component replacement applies through normal service life.

Past 200,000 km, expect front lower control arm bushings, stabilizer bar links, possibly upper strut mounts, valve cover gaskets and other seals. Total maintenance budget for a Vancouver or Calgary Outback past 200,000 km typically runs $1,200 to $2,000 annually if performed at independent Subaru specialists, or $2,000 to $3,500 at dealer service.

## Common questions

### Is a used Subaru Outback reliable past 200,000 km?

Yes, with disciplined maintenance. The 2016+ FB25 boxer engine routinely exceeds 250,000 km in Canadian service, and many examples reach 350,000+ km with regular oil changes and timing-component service. The Lineartronic [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) is the part that most needs looking after. Check that the fluid was changed on schedule before you buy, and keep doing it. At high mileage, an unbroken service record tells you more than the odometer.

### Do I need winter tires on a Subaru Outback in Canada?

Yes. Quebec law mandates winter tires from December 1 to March 15 under Highway Safety Code Article 440.1, and whatever the AWD system, it's the tires that decide your stopping distance. [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) helps the Outback get going in snow but does nothing for stopping. Braking depends on where the rubber meets the road, not on the drivetrain. Put Blizzaks or Michelin X-Ice on a separate set of winter wheels.

### Which Outback years should I avoid?

Pre-2013 Outbacks with the older EJ-series boxer engine have head gasket vulnerability that makes them poor used purchases in 2026. The first FB25-equipped Outbacks (2013) were part of the oil consumption problem that led to Subaru's extended warranty, so check the service records on early FB cars. The 2020 launch-year sixth-generation had documented teething issues addressed through software updates. The best buys are late fifth-generation cars (2018-2019) and sixth-generation cars from 2021 on.

### How much does CVT replacement cost on an Outback?

Lineartronic [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) replacement on an Outback runs $4,500 to $7,000 CAD installed at a Subaru dealer with new transmission, less with a remanufactured or used unit at independent Subaru specialists. That's why fluid changes matter: done on schedule, they prevent the failure that leads to replacement. A used Outback past 150,000 km with no record of CVT fluid service could be hiding a large bill.

### Outback vs CX-5: which is better in deep snow?

For absolute deep-snow capability and continuous variable-grip conditions, the Outback wins. Always-on [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) and 8.7 inches of ground clearance (7.6 on the CX-5) give it a clear edge once snow gets deeper than 6 to 8 inches. On plowed city commutes, a CX-5 with [i-Activ AWD](/glossary/awd/) and proper winter tires is nearly as good, and it uses less fuel and has a nicer cabin. Buy for the roads you drive most.

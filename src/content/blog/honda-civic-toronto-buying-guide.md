The Civic has been the best-selling passenger car in Canada for more than 25 consecutive years. That run started in 1998 and has not broken through five redesigns. Ontario is the country's biggest car market, and Honda builds the Civic and CR-V for North America in Alliston, about an hour north of Toronto.

That gives a Toronto buyer in 2026 more choice than anyone else in the country. This guide covers prices, OMVIC and the UVIP, the CVT problems on 2014-2017 cars, and which year-and-trim combinations are the best value right now.

## What does a used Honda Civic cost in Toronto in 2026?

Prices are fairly predictable. A 2017 Civic LX with 130,000 to 170,000 km in the GTA typically lists $14,000 to $17,500. A 2019 EX or Sport with 90,000 to 120,000 km lists $19,000 to $23,000. A 2021 Touring 1.5T with 60,000 to 90,000 km runs $24,000 to $28,000. Manual transmission 2018-2021 Si and Sport-trim hatchbacks cost $1,000 to $2,500 more than the same trim with the CVT, because from 2022 Honda offered the manual on far fewer Civics. The Type R is a different story. Production was limited, and clean used ones in Toronto routinely list above their original MSRP.

For a city this size, Toronto has fewer Civics for sale at any moment than you'd expect, because they sell so quickly. A clean LX or EX listing on japanauto.ca or AutoTrader frequently sells within 7 to 14 days at current 2026 inventory levels. Sport and Touring trims, particularly with under 100,000 km, can move in 3 to 5 days.

For most Toronto buyers, the 2017-2021 Sport is the trim to get. It has the 1.5L L15 turbo with the CVT (or a manual through 2021), plus a sportier front end than the lower trims. The Touring is the comfortable top trim, with leather, full Honda Sensing and the best cabin tech of the regular Civics. The Type R, with its 2.0L K20C turbo, is a different car altogether.

## Which Civic generation and trim should Toronto buyers target?

Most 2026 used buyers should focus on the 2016-2021 tenth-generation Civic. This generation introduced the 1.5L L15 turbo to the Civic line, which delivered better fuel economy and stronger torque than the previous generation's naturally aspirated 1.8L. Early cars (2016-2018) had a cold-weather oil-dilution problem that Honda Canada fixed with a software update and an extended warranty, so ask whether that was done. The Si (a higher-output 1.5L turbo) and the Type R (2.0L K20C [i-VTEC](/glossary/i-vtec/) turbo), both manual-only, are enthusiast cars and need their own guide.

The 2022-onward eleventh-generation Civic is the current production. Used pricing on the eleventh-generation in 2026 typically runs $4,000 to $7,000 above the equivalent 2020-2021 tenth-generation, For most buyers the cheaper late tenth-generation car is the better buy. Interior tech is close, the powertrain is much the same, and Honda had ironed out the build quality by those final years.

Skip the 2014-2015 ninth-generation Civic unless the budget truly won't stretch. The 1.8L R18 four-cylinder is durable, but the chassis and interior are dated, and these were the first Civics with Honda's CVT, which had the shudder bulletins covered below.

## Is a private sale or OMVIC-registered dealer better in Ontario?

Ontario gives you two safety nets: [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) registration for dealers, and the [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/) for private sales. A buyer working with an OMVIC-registered dealer gets disclosure obligations under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act, plus access to the Motor Vehicle Dealers Compensation Fund (up to $45,000 per claim). Check OMVIC registration at omvic.ca before you pay. Ontario dealers have to advertise under their registered name, including in online listings on japanauto.ca.

For a private sale, the UVIP is mandatory. The seller must obtain it from ServiceOntario for $20 and provide it at the point of sale. The UVIP shows Ontario registration history, current lien status, and the wholesale value the Ministry of Transportation will use to calculate Retail Sales Tax at registration. Without a UVIP, the buyer cannot register the vehicle. You can also order a UVIP yourself for any Ontario-registered vehicle through ServiceOntario before you start negotiating. At $20, it's usually worth it.

The premium for an OMVIC-registered dealer purchase versus a comparable private listing is typically $1,500 to $3,000. That premium buys you the regulator's protection. If you've bought privately before and know what to check, you may not need it. If this is your first used car, pay it.

## How does the Civic handle Toronto winters and salt-belt conditions?

Every regular Civic is front-wheel drive, and on proper winter tires that's plenty for a GTA winter. People in Toronto, Mississauga, Markham, Oakville and Brampton have driven FWD Civics through decades of winters. What you need is winter tires on their own set of wheels, not AWD.

Civics have a decent record against salt. Modern (2014+) Honda body panels use galvanized steel and clear-coat formulations that hold up well against Ontario road salt. The 2006-2015 eighth- and ninth-generation Civics have more documented rust on rocker panels and rear wheel well lips on cars that lived multiple winters in Ontario without undercoating. The 2016+ tenth generation is less affected. On any private-sale Civic, make sure the PPI includes a proper look underneath, especially if the rocker panel undercoating has lifted or shows surface rust.

Of all the rust problems, corroded brake lines are the one that affects how the car drives. Toronto road salt corrodes rear brake lines on a typical Civic timeline of 8 to 12 years. Replacement of corroded brake lines runs $300 to $700 depending on extent. Verify brake line condition during PPI on any Civic past 8 years from production date.

## What should the pre-purchase inspection check on a Civic?

Five things matter most on a used Civic. First, run the [VIN](/glossary/vin/) through the recall search at defects.tc.gc.ca. Honda Canada has issued recalls on certain Civic production years for fuel pump issues and electrical components, and an open recall is repairable for free at any Honda dealer regardless of vehicle age or current ownership. Second, check the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) fluid history on any 2014-2017 Civic LX or Sport. Honda issued service bulletins for shudder under part-throttle and cold-shift judder, which were addressed through software updates and fluid revisions. Verify fluid change history at the manufacturer-specified interval before purchase.

Third, look at the brakes. Toronto road salt eats rear brake lines, calipers and pad backing plates, and replacing corroded parts can run $400 to $800. Fourth, check suspension wear on front lower control arm bushings, stabilizer bar links, and rear trailing arm bushings, which wear faster on Toronto's rougher roads than they would in Vancouver or Calgary. Fifth, inspect the underside for rust, particularly on rocker panels and rear subframe attachment points if the vehicle has spent multiple winters in salt-belt provinces.

A thorough PPI at an independent Japanese-car specialist on Eglinton Avenue West, in Mississauga, or in Scarborough takes one to two and a half hours and costs $150 to $300. Always get one on a private sale. If an OMVIC-registered dealer includes a safety certificate, you can usually skip it, though on an expensive car it's still worth doing.

## What taxes and fees apply to a used Civic purchase in Ontario?

Ontario taxes used cars at 13 percent either way, but the tax works differently. A dealer charges 13 percent HST on the selling price. On a private sale you pay 13 percent retail sales tax (RST) at ServiceOntario when you register the car, and it's calculated on the higher of the actual sale price or the wholesale value listed on the [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/). This means a private-sale Civic bought for $18,000 with a UVIP wholesale value of $20,000 will be taxed on the $20,000 figure, not the lower negotiated price. Look at the UVIP wholesale value before you negotiate.

On top of the tax, expect registration fees of approximately $90 at ServiceOntario, the $20 UVIP cost (paid by seller on private sales, by buyer if ordered independently), Safety Standards Certificate cost of $80 to $150 plus any required repairs, and any plate transfer or new-plate fees that apply. A typical $20,000 private-sale Civic transaction in Toronto runs roughly $22,700 to $23,000 all-in including RST, registration, and safety.

## What are the most common Civic repair costs after 150,000 km?

The 1.5L L15 turbo and the modern CVT hold up well, but between 150,000 and 200,000 km the usual wear items come due. Budget $300 to $500 for front brake pads and rotors, $300 to $450 for rear pads and rotors, $400 to $700 for brake-line replacement on Toronto-driven examples, $300 to $500 for stabilizer bar links and lower control arm bushings, and $300 to $500 for spark plug replacement at the manufacturer interval.

Past 200,000 km, the timing chain (the modern Civic has a chain, not a belt) can develop tensioner wear that produces cold-start rattle. Repair runs $1,000 to $1,500 at independent shops, more at the dealer. The CVT requires fluid changes at the manufacturer-specified interval (typically 60,000 to 90,000 km on Civic applications). If the catalytic converter goes, expect $1,000 to $1,800 with [OEM](/glossary/oem/)-equivalent parts from Denso or NGK. Our [OEM glossary entry](/glossary/oem/) explains those suppliers.

## Common questions

### Is a used Honda Civic Sport a good buy in Toronto in 2026?

For most buyers, yes. A 2017-2021 Civic Sport with the 1.5L L15 turbo (ideally a 2019 or later, past the oil-dilution fix), CVT or manual, gives you good value and a fun drive, and it's easy to sell later. A 2019 Sport with 90,000 to 120,000 km at $19,000 to $22,000 in the GTA is a sound buy. Verify [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) registration on dealer transactions, demand a [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/) on private sales, and confirm CVT fluid history on any 2017 Sport (the year service bulletins were addressed). Manual-transmission Sport trims are increasingly rare and command modest premiums.

### How much tax do I pay on a used Civic in Ontario?

13 percent either way. On a private sale it's retail sales tax (RST), paid at ServiceOntario when you register, on the higher of the actual sale price or the wholesale value listed on the [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/). For a $20,000 private-sale Civic with a UVIP wholesale value of $20,500, RST is calculated on $20,500, which comes to $2,665. The same car from an OMVIC-registered dealer carries 13 percent HST on the dealer's selling price. Total all-in cost on a $20,000 private Civic transaction typically runs $22,700 to $23,000 including RST, registration ($90), UVIP ($20), and safety certificate ($80-$150).

### Should I worry about the CVT on a used Civic?

For 2014-2017 Civic LX and Sport applications, [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) service bulletins addressed shudder under part-throttle and cold-shift judder through software updates and fluid revisions. Verify fluid change history at the manufacturer-specified interval (60,000 to 90,000 km) before purchase on any 2014-2017 example. From 2018 the CVT is much improved and the problem rarely shows up. Either way, a CVT lives or dies by its fluid changes, and those matter more than the odometer. A clean fluid history on a 130,000 km Civic is a stronger signal than missing service records on a 90,000 km Civic.

### Which year of Civic is most reliable?

The 2018-2021 tenth-generation Civic, especially the Sport and Si, is the most reliable used Civic you can buy in Toronto today. The 1.5L L15 turbo (used in the Sport, Touring and Si) is mature by that production window, the oil-dilution fix was in place, the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) issues from earlier in the generation were largely addressed, and the cabin tech is competitive with current production. The 2006-2011 eighth-generation Civic with the 1.8L R18 is also exceptionally durable, but availability of clean examples in 2026 is limited. The 2022-onward eleventh generation is excellent, but its reliability edge over a late tenth-gen car is small and doesn't justify the extra cost.

### How do I check the UVIP before buying a private Civic?

Order the [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/) directly from ServiceOntario at $20, either online through the ServiceOntario website by entering the [VIN](/glossary/vin/) and licence plate, or in person at any ServiceOntario location. The seller is legally required to provide the UVIP at point of sale on a private transaction, but you can also order independently as a due-diligence step before negotiating. It shows Ontario registration history, current lien status, and the wholesale value used to calculate your retail sales tax, which is useful to know before you agree on a price.

If you're watching for a particular Civic, set up a saved-search alert on japanauto.ca. Most clean GTA Civics sell in under 14 days at current 2026 inventory levels, and the best trims (Sport, Touring, Si, Type R) go in days.

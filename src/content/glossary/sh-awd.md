## What is SH-AWD?

SH-AWD stands for Super Handling All-Wheel Drive. It is Acura's torque-vectoring all-wheel drive system, introduced on the 2005 Acura RL and now standard on every trim of the Canadian-spec RDX and the 2021-and-newer TLX, and on the current MDX. Most reactive AWD systems only move torque between the front and rear axles. SH-AWD also moves it between the left and right rear wheels, using electronically controlled clutch packs in the rear differential.

What you get is active torque vectoring in corners. When the system reads a steering input, it can send up to 70 percent of available torque to the rear axle and then push most of that to the outer rear wheel. The resulting yaw moment rotates the car into the turn. That behaves more like a sport-handling differential than a typical AWD coupling, and it is why the MDX and RDX corner with noticeably more agility than rival luxury crossovers.

The fourth-generation MDX (2022 model year on) uses the latest version, with more rear-bias capability than the previous MDX. In the TLX Type S and A-Spec, SH-AWD sits under a sport sedan aimed at the BMW M340i xDrive and Audi S4. The RDX A-Spec pairs it with the 2.0L turbo K20C [i-VTEC](/glossary/i-vtec/) engine.

## Why it matters in Canada

Few AWD systems on any Japanese-brand vehicle sold in Canada are as sophisticated. In Canadian winter conditions, with grip changing from one wheel to the next, continuous engagement and rear torque vectoring give it a measurable edge over the reactive AWD on most Lexus, Infiniti, and German luxury rivals. It won't match Subaru's [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) in deep snow, since the Subaru chassis is built differently. On plowed city roads with proper winter tires, though, SH-AWD is among the best in the luxury crossover class.

That helps at resale, especially in snow-belt cities where many luxury buyers won't consider a vehicle without AWD.

## Common questions

### What is the difference between SH-AWD and regular AWD?

Most Japanese crossovers and luxury vehicles split torque only front to rear, usually with an electronically controlled clutch that engages the rear axle once sensors detect slip. SH-AWD adds a second split, left to right across the rear wheels, using electronically controlled clutch packs in the rear differential. Sending extra torque to the outer rear wheel in a corner creates a yaw moment that turns the vehicle in, and the car feels more agile than one with conventional AWD.

### Is SH-AWD better than Subaru Symmetrical AWD?

Depends what you measure. For cornering and torque-vectoring response, SH-AWD wins: the left-right rear torque split gives a sporty feel that [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) can't match. For deep snow and constantly shifting grip, Symmetrical AWD wins, because the boxer engine and longitudinal layout give the chassis different dynamics suited to that job. For winter commuting on plowed roads in a major Canadian city, both are fully capable on proper winter tires. The choice usually comes down to priorities: a luxury cabin and sharp handling (SH-AWD on an Acura) or ruggedness and outdoor utility (Symmetrical AWD on a Subaru).

### Which Acura models have SH-AWD?

[SH-AWD](/glossary/awd/) is standard on every trim of the current Canadian-market Acura MDX and RDX, and on every 2021-and-newer TLX, including the 2.0L turbo and the Type S. The Acura Integra (2023+), Type S included, is front-wheel drive only and doesn't offer SH-AWD. Older Acuras vary, so check the specific trim on a used one. The second-generation NSX (2017-2022) used a hybrid SH-AWD that is mechanically different from the crossover system. For most Canadian Acura shoppers, the MDX is the SH-AWD vehicle to look at.

## What is a hybrid?

A hybrid vehicle combines an internal-combustion engine with one or more electric motors running off a battery. Two things charge that battery. One is regenerative braking: as you slow down, the motor runs in reverse as a generator and turns the car's motion back into electricity. The other is the gasoline engine, when needed. There is no charging port and no plug. A hybrid looks after itself and fills up at the gas pump like any other car.

There are three layouts. A series hybrid uses its gasoline engine only as a generator, and the engine never drives the wheels; Honda's [i-MMD](/glossary/i-mmd/) works this way at city speeds. A parallel hybrid runs engine and electric motor side by side through a shared transmission. Series-parallel hybrids are the main Japanese layout. They use a power-split device, with Toyota's [e-CVT](/glossary/e-cvt/) as the textbook example, to blend engine and motor torque smoothly according to speed and load.

The two best-selling hybrid systems in Canada are Toyota's Hybrid Synergy Drive and Honda's i-MMD/e:HEV. Toyota badges its system plain "Hybrid" on the Camry, RAV4, Highlander, Corolla, Prius and Sienna, and Lexus uses related systems across its hybrid line. Honda's is in the CR-V Hybrid, the Accord Hybrid and the Civic Hybrid, which returned for the 2025 model year.

## Why it matters in Canada

Conventional hybrids don't qualify for Canada's federal EV incentive. The old iZEV rebate ended in January 2025, and its replacement, the Electric Vehicle Affordability Program (new vehicles bought or leased from February 16, 2026), covers Battery Electric, Plug-in Hybrid and Fuel Cell vehicles only. A car without a charging port is out. Some Canadian buyers assume "hybrid" means "rebate-eligible", so this trips people up. A new Toyota Camry Hybrid gets nothing. A new plug-in [PHEV](/glossary/phev/) can, if it meets the price cap.

For buyers in Toronto, Vancouver or Montreal with no way to charge at home, a hybrid still makes practical sense. The fuel savings in stop-and-go traffic are real, typically 25 to 40 percent better than the gasoline version in city driving, and the driver doesn't have to change any habits. Winter does cost you: expect 15 to 25 percent worse fuel economy at -20°C than the rating. The hybrid system itself is not bothered by the cold.

Per Canadian Black Book data, Toyota and Honda hybrids have held their value better than their gas-only equivalents for more than ten years. Part of the reason is that the [e-CVT](/glossary/e-cvt/) and [i-MMD](/glossary/i-mmd/) transmissions are mechanically tougher than conventional automatics.

## Common questions

### What is the difference between a hybrid and a plug-in hybrid?

A conventional hybrid charges its battery only through regenerative braking and the gasoline engine. It has no plug. The battery is small (typically 1 to 2 kWh) and gives short bursts of electric-only driving, mostly at low speed. A [Plug-in Hybrid](/glossary/phev/) (PHEV) carries a much bigger battery, typically 8 to 18 kWh, that you charge from an outlet. That gives 30 to 80 km on electricity before the gas engine starts. New PHEVs can qualify for Canada's federal EV incentive. Conventional hybrids can't.

### Do hybrids work well in Canadian winters?

Yes. Winter doesn't harm the hybrid powertrain. The [e-CVT](/glossary/e-cvt/) in Toyota's Hybrid Synergy Drive vehicles has two decades of Canadian fleet use behind it in Toronto, Montreal, Calgary and Edmonton. Fuel economy does drop, measurably: plan on 15 to 25 percent higher litres-per-100-km at -20°C than the NRCan combined rating. Toyota Canada covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 240,000 km on 2020 and newer models (8 years or 160,000 km before that), and the coverage carries over to later owners. Honda's hybrid coverage terms differ, so check them for the specific model year.

### How long do hybrid batteries last?

In a well-kept Toyota or Honda, the traction battery usually lasts a very long time, and high-mileage hybrid taxis are the usual proof. If you do need one, get quotes from a dealer and from an independent hybrid-battery shop, since refurbished packs cost much less than new. On a 2020 or newer Toyota, the 10-year / 240,000 km battery warranty covers most failures a typical buyer is likely to see.

### Is the Toyota hybrid system the same as the Honda one?

No. Toyota's Hybrid Synergy Drive uses an [e-CVT](/glossary/e-cvt/): a single planetary gearset linking the gasoline engine to two motor-generators, with software varying motor speeds to give a continuously variable ratio. Honda's [i-MMD](/glossary/i-mmd/) (also called e:HEV) uses two separate motors plus a clutch that locks the engine straight to the wheels at highway cruising speeds. Real-world fuel economy ends up similar. Toyota's system is mechanically simpler, and Honda's is more efficient at a steady highway speed.

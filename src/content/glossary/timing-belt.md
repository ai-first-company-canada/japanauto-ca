## What is a timing belt?

A timing belt is a toothed, reinforced rubber belt that keeps an engine's crankshaft and camshafts turning in sync. The camshafts decide when the intake and exhaust valves open and close relative to each piston, so the timing has to be exact. Let the belt slip a few teeth and the valves and pistons fall out of phase. On most modern Japanese engines, an open valve then meets a rising piston hard enough to bend the valve and damage the cylinder head.

Engines that get damaged this way are called "interference engines." Most modern Japanese engines built for fuel economy are interference designs. That includes the Honda J-series V6 (J35) in the Odyssey, Pilot, and Acura MDX, and many older Honda four-cylinders. (Toyota's 2GR-FE V6, found in many Camry, RAV4, Highlander and Sienna models, uses a timing chain, not a belt.) Interference designs allow tighter valve clearances and higher compression ratios, which helps efficiency. The price is severe damage if the timing part fails.

Many Japanese manufacturers switched from belts to timing chains during the 2000s. Toyota's A25A four-cylinder, Honda's K20C and 1.5L L15 turbo, and most Mazda [SkyActiv](/glossary/skyactiv/) engines now run chains, which last the life of the engine with no scheduled replacement. Some Honda V6 (J35) engines in the Odyssey, Pilot, and Acura MDX still use belts.

## Why it matters in Canada

On certain models, timing-belt history is the first thing a Canadian used buyer should check. The Honda J35 V6 belt interval is about 168,000 km (sooner if the Maintenance Minder or the owner's manual age limit says so), and that engine powers the Odyssey, Pilot, and Acura MDX. A 2018 Pilot at 175,000 km with no record of belt service carries $1,200 to $1,800 in deferred maintenance, plus a real chance of engine failure if the belt has stretched or worn. Put the same Pilot next to records showing a new belt at 145,000 km and you are looking at a very different car.

Belts also turn up on some older Subaru EJ-series engines, some pre-2010 Mitsubishi V6s, and certain older Toyota V6s from before the GR family. On any used Japanese vehicle from about 2010 or earlier, find out whether it has a belt or a chain, then check the service history to match. Anything built after about 2015 almost certainly has a chain.

## Common questions

### Does my Japanese car have a timing belt or timing chain?

Most modern Japanese engines (typically post-2015 production) use chains. The main exception is the Honda J-series 3.5L V6 in the Odyssey, Pilot, Ridgeline, and Acura MDX, which uses a belt replaced at about 168,000 km. Toyota's 2GR-FE V6 (2005 onward) has a chain; some older Toyota V6s, such as the 1MZ-FE, used belts. Many pre-2010 Honda four-cylinders, Subaru EJ-series boxers, and Mitsubishi V6s used belts too. For a definite answer on a specific car, check the owner's manual or the maker's online service guide, or call a same-brand dealer's parts counter with the [VIN](/glossary/vin/).

### When should a timing belt be replaced on a Honda?

On the Honda J-series V6, at about 168,000 km (105,000 miles), or sooner if the Maintenance Minder or the age limit in your owner's manual comes first. That covers the Odyssey, Pilot, Ridgeline, and Acura MDX with the 3.5L V6. The job replaces the belt plus the water pump (the belt drives it, so nearly everyone changes both together), the tensioner, and the idler pulleys. At a Honda or Acura dealer, expect $1,200 to $1,800 CAD installed. Independent shops that know Hondas usually charge a little less. Skipping the service or running past the interval puts the engine at risk if the belt lets go.

### What happens if a timing belt breaks?

On an interference engine (the Honda J35 V6 and many older Honda four-cylinders, for example), open valves collide with rising pistons. The valves bend, and the cylinder head and pistons are often damaged as well. The fix is usually a full engine rebuild ($4,000 to $7,000 CAD on a Honda V6) or a used or remanufactured engine ($3,500 to $6,000 plus labour). A non-interference engine, rare in modern Japanese production, just stops running, and a new belt fixes it. Most interference designs give you no such luck.

### How much does timing belt service cost in Canada?

On a Honda J-series V6, a Honda or Acura dealer typically charges $1,200 to $1,800 CAD installed, covering the belt, water pump, tensioner, idler pulleys, and labour. Independent Japanese-car shops in Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, and Montreal often do the same job for $900 to $1,400 with [OEM](/glossary/oem/)-equivalent parts. On Subaru EJ-series boxers (older Outback, Forester, Legacy), belt service usually runs $900 to $1,400, and older Toyota and Mitsubishi V6s land in about the same range. Modern Japanese engines with timing chains don't need this scheduled service at all.

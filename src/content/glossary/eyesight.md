## What is EyeSight?

EyeSight is Subaru's driver assistance system, introduced to North America on 2013 model-year vehicles. Most competing systems rely mainly on radar, lidar or ultrasonic sensors. EyeSight uses two colour cameras mounted side by side at the windshield. With two viewpoints it can triangulate depth, which is how human eyes judge distance too.

It bundles five functions:

- Adaptive cruise control holds a set following distance behind the car ahead.
- Pre-collision braking applies the brakes automatically if a crash is coming and the driver hasn't reacted.
- Lane departure and sway warning beeps and flashes when the car drifts out of its lane without a signal.
- Lane keep assist steers gently to keep the car centred.
- Lead-vehicle start alert tells you when stopped traffic ahead has started moving.

The newest generation of EyeSight reached North America on top trims of the 2023 Outback and became standard on the 2024 Crosstrek and Impreza. It adds a wide-angle mono camera for better coverage at intersections and works with an electric brake booster for faster automatic braking. It is standard on most Canadian-spec Subaru Outback, Forester, Crosstrek, Impreza, Legacy and Ascent trims.

## Why it matters in Canada

Subaru Canada makes EyeSight standard on more trims than most competitors do. Most Outback, Forester and Crosstrek trims have it even at the entry price. The IIHS Top Safety Pick+ award requires advanced front crash prevention among other criteria, and EyeSight-equipped Subarus have won it across several model lines and years. That rating carries real weight in resale, especially with family buyers in Toronto, Vancouver and Calgary.

Heavy snow, fog or extreme rain can blind the cameras to lane markings and other vehicles. When EyeSight can't see the road well enough, it shuts off adaptive cruise and pre-collision braking and shows a warning light. Any windshield replacement, or a chip repair near the camera bracket, means the system has to be recalibrated at a Subaru dealer or a qualified glass shop. If a used Subaru has had windshield work, ask for proof of recalibration.

## Common questions

### What is Subaru EyeSight?

Subaru's driver assistance system. Two colour cameras mounted side by side at the windshield judge depth and recognize vehicles, pedestrians, lane lines and brake lights. It provides adaptive cruise control, pre-collision braking, lane departure warning, lane keep assist and a lead-vehicle start alert. Unlike radar-based systems from other brands, it works from cameras alone, and EyeSight-equipped Subarus across several model lines have earned IIHS Top Safety Pick+ ratings.

### Does EyeSight work in snow?

In light to moderate snow, when you can still see well, yes. That covers most Canadian winter commutes. In heavy snow, freezing rain or dense fog, the cameras lose a clear view and performance drops. When the system can't read the road ahead reliably, it switches off adaptive cruise and pre-collision braking and puts a warning on the dash. You stay in full control of the car at all times. EyeSight assists the driver and does not stand in for one.

### Is EyeSight standard on all Subaru models in Canada?

Not all of them. It is standard on most trims of the Canadian-market Outback, Forester, Crosstrek, Impreza, Legacy and Ascent, entry-level models included. The BRZ sports coupe and manual-transmission WRX trims have historically gone without it, though EyeSight is now offered on 2022+ WRX automatic trims. Most competitors keep comparable driver assistance for mid or top trims, so Subaru's standard coverage is wider. Check equipment by trim and model year before buying a used Subaru.

## What is the VSA?

The Vehicle Sales Authority of British Columbia (VSA) is the delegated administrative authority that regulates retail motor-vehicle dealers and salespersons in BC. It was established in 2004 and works under the Motor Dealer Act and the Motor Dealer Customer Compensation Fund Regulation. Like Ontario's [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) and Alberta's [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/), it is a not-for-profit body that sits at arm's length from the provincial government and licenses, supervises, and disciplines the car retail trade.

The VSA licenses every motor dealer and salesperson in British Columbia and enforces the Motor Dealer Act and its regulations. It runs the mandatory salesperson certification course. It also operates the BC Motor Dealer Customer Compensation Fund, a pool paid for by the industry that reimburses consumers for verifiable losses caused by VSA-licensed dealers.

Licensing applies to new- and used-car sellers alike. Any dealer selling used Japanese vehicles in Vancouver, Victoria, Surrey, Burnaby, or elsewhere in BC needs a current VSA licence. The licence number has to appear in its advertising, online listings on japanauto.ca included, and every retail sale has to meet the Motor Dealer Act's disclosure rules.

## Why it matters in Canada

Vancouver is one of the main markets on japanauto.ca and one of Canada's largest [JDM](/glossary/jdm/)-import hubs. Vehicles shipped from Japan arrive through the Port of Vancouver, on roll-on/roll-off car carriers that use the Fraser River auto terminals such as Annacis Island, or in containers. From there they go to BC dealers or onto trucks bound for buyers elsewhere in Western Canada. Any BC dealer handling those imports needs a current VSA licence, and VSA disclosure rules cover every retail sale.

BC's Motor Dealer Customer Compensation Fund works much like the OMVIC and AMVIC funds. Each provincial fund is separate and pays claims only against dealers licensed in its own province. A BC consumer who lost money to an Ontario dealer would have to claim against the OMVIC fund in Ontario, not the BC fund. Buying across provincial lines gets complicated, which is one reason BC buyers are usually told to stick with VSA-licensed BC dealers.

The VSA dealer search at mvsabc.com lets you check a dealer's licence status, registered business address, and disciplinary record before you buy.

## Common questions

### Is the VSA the same as OMVIC?

No. The VSA covers British Columbia under the Motor Dealer Act. [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) covers Ontario under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act, 2002, and [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/) covers Alberta under the Consumer Protection Act. Each regulator works on its own: separate licences, a separate compensation fund, its own disclosure rules and fees. They cooperate informally on cross-border cases but share no licensing authority. A VSA licence carries no standing in Ontario or Alberta, so a BC dealer needs separate licences to trade there.

### How do I check a BC car dealer's licence?

Use the VSA dealer search at mvsabc.com. Enter the dealer's legal business name, trade name, or the VSA licence number from its ad. The results show licence status (active, expired, suspended, or revoked), the expiry date, the registered business address, and any public disciplinary action from the past several years. If a dealer can't give you a licence number, or the number doesn't match an active record, walk away. Every VSA-licensed dealer must show its licence number in its ads, including online listings on japanauto.ca.

### Does the BC Motor Dealer Compensation Fund cover private sales?

No. The fund, run by the VSA, only reimburses verifiable losses caused by VSA-licensed dealers. The Motor Dealer Act applies only to licensed dealers, so private sales fall outside both the VSA's oversight and the fund. A private buyer has to do the checking personally: a [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) report, a [Lien](/glossary/lien/) search through the BC Personal Property Registry, an ICBC Vehicle Claims History Report, and a pre-purchase inspection.

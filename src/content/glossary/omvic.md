## What is OMVIC?

OMVIC stands for the Ontario Motor Vehicle Industry Council. It is the delegated administrative authority that regulates every motor vehicle dealer and salesperson in Ontario under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act, 2002. Alberta's [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/) and British Columbia's [VSA](/glossary/vsa/) work the same way: each province has handed oversight of car retail to a not-for-profit body at arm's length from government.

OMVIC dates from 1997. It licenses every motor-vehicle dealer and salesperson in Ontario and runs the mandatory salesperson course (the Automotive Certification Course). It also enforces the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act. And it operates the Motor Vehicle Dealers Compensation Fund, an industry-funded pool that can reimburse consumers for proven losses caused by registered dealers, up to $45,000 per claim.

Registration applies to new- and used-car dealers alike. A dealer selling used Japanese cars in Toronto, Ottawa, Hamilton or anywhere else in Ontario needs an active OMVIC registration. It has to show the registration number in its advertising, online listings on japanauto.ca included, and follow OMVIC's disclosure rules on every retail sale.

## Why it matters in Canada

Toronto and Ottawa are two of the main markets japanauto.ca covers, and every dealer listing there must be OMVIC-registered. To check a dealer, use the search tool at omvic.ca. It shows the legal business name, registration status (active, expired or suspended), expiry date, registered address and any disciplinary history from the past several years. It takes less than a minute, and it's the first thing to do before any deal with an Ontario dealer.

OMVIC does not regulate private sellers. The Motor Vehicle Dealers Act covers registered dealers only, so someone selling their own car is outside its reach. The [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/) fills that gap on private sales. It gives the buyer the registry and lien data that OMVIC's disclosure rules would require a dealer to provide.

Alberta's [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/) runs a similar consumer compensation fund, and BC has the Motor Dealer Customer Compensation Fund run by the [VSA](/glossary/vsa/). Each pays up to its own provincial cap per claim. OMVIC's $45,000 cap is more than double BC's $20,000 limit.

## Common questions

### Is OMVIC the same as AMVIC?

No. They are separate provincial regulators. OMVIC covers Ontario under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act, 2002. [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/) covers Alberta under the Consumer Protection Act and the Automotive Business Regulation. Each issues its own licences, runs its own compensation fund, and sets its own disclosure rules and fees. A dealer licensed in Ontario has no standing in Alberta and needs a separate AMVIC licence to sell there. OMVIC, AMVIC and the [VSA](/glossary/vsa/) cooperate informally on cross-border cases but share no licensing or enforcement powers.

### How much does the OMVIC compensation fund cover?

Up to $45,000 CAD per claim. The Motor Vehicle Dealers Compensation Fund repays Ontario consumers for proven financial losses caused by an OMVIC-registered dealer. Typical cases include undisclosed liens, a vehicle never delivered after a deposit, and misrepresentation that OMVIC upholds after investigating. It doesn't cover private sales, sales by unlicensed sellers, or losses where the consumer skipped OMVIC's dispute-resolution channels. To file, you need proof of the transaction, proof of the loss, and evidence that you first tried to resolve it with the dealer.

### Do private sellers need to register with OMVIC?

No. The Motor Vehicle Dealers Act applies only to registered dealers, and someone selling their own car privately doesn't need to register. What counts is intent to profit and how often you sell. Selling your own car now and then is unregulated, but selling more than a handful a year (typically treated as "for profit") may require dealer registration. Private buyers get no OMVIC protection, so lean on the [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/), [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) and a pre-purchase inspection.

### How do I check if an Ontario car dealer is registered with OMVIC?

Use the dealer search tool at omvic.ca. Enter the dealer's legal business name, trade name, or the OMVIC registration number from its ad. You'll see the registration status (active, expired, suspended or revoked), expiry date, registered address and any public disciplinary actions from the past several years. If the dealer can't give you a registration number, or the number doesn't match an active record, walk away. Every registered dealer has to show its number in ads, online listings included.

This page reflects Ontario rules in force as of May 2026. For binding advice on your own purchase, talk to a lawyer; OMVIC itself can explain how the rules apply to a registered dealer.

## What is Symmetrical AWD?

Symmetrical All-Wheel Drive is Subaru's full-time AWD system, built around a powertrain laid out lengthwise along the vehicle's centreline. The boxer engine, whose pistons move horizontally in opposed pairs instead of vertically as in an inline or V engine, sits low in the chassis on that lengthwise axis. The transmission, transfer case, driveshafts, and rear differential line up symmetrically along the same axis.

That layout pays off in winter. With mass spread evenly along the car's length, the polar moment of inertia around the vertical axis is lower, so the car corners more predictably. The boxer's low centre of gravity cuts body roll. Torque also goes to all four wheels all the time. Subaru's system does not wait for slip and then engage, so there is none of the sensor-to-actuator delay found in reactive systems.

Most Canadian Subarus pair Symmetrical AWD with a Lineartronic [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) and Active Torque Split, which uses an electronically controlled multi-plate clutch to manage the front/rear split. Manual-transmission WRX trims use a viscous-coupling centre differential. The BRZ sports coupe is rear-wheel drive only, the one Subaru sold in Canada without Symmetrical AWD.

## Why it matters in Canada

Subaru is a common sight in Canada's snowiest regions (Quebec, Atlantic Canada, Northern Ontario, the BC interior), and Symmetrical AWD is the main reason. Grip in a Canadian winter changes unpredictably from wheel to wheel. A system that is always engaged handles that better than one that has to wait for slip.

It helps resale too. Used Outbacks, Foresters and Crosstreks with good service records sell readily in winter markets. For rural Quebec buyers and commuters in Calgary's chinook zone, the AWD is the reason they buy the car.

## Common questions

### What makes Subaru's AWD different from other AWD systems?

It is always engaged, sending torque to all four wheels instead of reacting once slip is detected. Most competing AWD systems on Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Mazda, and the Japanese luxury brands drive mainly the front wheels (or the rear, on luxury sport sedans) and bring in the other axle through an electronically controlled clutch when sensors pick up wheel slip. That engagement takes milliseconds, but the design is still reactive. On a Subaru, the torque is already at the wheels before slip starts, with no sensor delay.

### Is Symmetrical AWD always on?

Yes, on every Canadian-market Subaru except the BRZ sports coupe, which is rear-wheel drive only. (Japan-domestic Subaru kei vehicles, not sold here, are also exceptions.) The Outback, Forester, Crosstrek, Impreza, Legacy, Ascent, and WRX all come standard with continuously engaged Symmetrical AWD, and there is no switch to turn it off. Torque reaches all four wheels at all times, and the split shifts with conditions, electronically through Active Torque Split or mechanically through the viscous-coupling centre differential on manual WRXs.

### Does the Subaru BRZ have Symmetrical AWD?

No. The BRZ is a rear-wheel-drive sports coupe developed with Toyota, which sells it as the GR86. The shared chassis and powertrain were tuned for handling balance and low weight, not all-weather grip, so both cars are rear-wheel drive only. It is the one Subaru sold in Canada without Symmetrical AWD. Canadian buyers who want an AWD Subaru sports car should look at the WRX, which keeps Symmetrical AWD with a viscous-coupling centre differential on manual trims and Active Torque Split on automatic trims.

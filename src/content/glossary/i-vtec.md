## What is i-VTEC?

i-VTEC stands for intelligent Variable Valve Timing and Lift Electronic Control. Honda introduced it in 2001. It combines two mechanisms: the original VTEC, which switches between two or three fixed cam profiles at a set RPM, and a cam-phasing system that keeps adjusting intake camshaft timing relative to the crankshaft.

Plain VTEC first appeared on the 1989 Integra. At high RPM it switched cam lobes mechanically for more aggressive valve timing, the well-known "VTEC kick" that made performance Hondas famous. It worked like an on/off switch, though: one cam profile for low revs, another for high revs, with a transition zone in between. i-VTEC put variable intake-cam phasing on top of that. Low-RPM running got smoother, emissions and low-end torque improved, and the top-end power stayed.

Most modern Honda and Acura engines sold in Canada use i-VTEC, among them the Civic 2.0L K20C, CR-V 1.5T L15B and the 2.0T K20C4 used in the 2018 to 2022 Accord. The Civic Type R and Acura Integra Type S pair a more aggressive i-VTEC tune with turbocharging.

## Why it matters in Canada

Honda Canada has been putting i-VTEC engines in volume models since 2002, so few variable-valve systems have more Canadian kilometres behind them. That record helps the resale value of the Civic and CR-V, two of the most common used Japanese cars in Canada. The mechanism is simple and runs on oil pressure, and well-maintained i-VTEC engines regularly see high mileage.

For [JDM](/glossary/jdm/) Honda imports, K-series i-VTEC versus older B-series VTEC matters when you order parts. The K-series uses a different cylinder head design, and most B-series VTEC parts won't swap across. A 2000 Integra Type R (DC2) brought in from Japan under the 15-year rule has B-series VTEC. A 2002 Integra Type R (DC5) or a 2008 Civic Type R (FD2) has K-series i-VTEC. Check the engine code against the [VIN](/glossary/vin/) before you order anything.

## Common questions

### What is the difference between VTEC and i-VTEC?

VTEC switches between two or three fixed camshaft profiles at a set RPM, giving more aggressive valve timing up high. i-VTEC adds continuously variable intake-cam phasing, which helps low-RPM torque and emissions and leaves the high-RPM power intact. VTEC dates from 1989 and was Honda's standard system through the late 1990s. i-VTEC followed in 2001 and has been on virtually every Honda and Acura engine sold in Canada since the early 2000s.

### Is i-VTEC reliable?

Yes. The mechanism is simple and oil-pressure driven, and well-maintained i-VTEC engines regularly see high mileage across several engine generations. The turbocharged 2.0L K20C family in the Civic Type R and in Acura's RDX and TLX has a good reputation so far. (The current Civic Si uses the 1.5L turbo L15, not the K20C.) Failures are rare, and when they happen the cause is usually poor oil maintenance, not the design. Stick to the oil grade and change interval Honda specifies.

### Does the Honda Civic in Canada have i-VTEC?

Yes. Every Civic sold in Canada from the 2006 model year on has i-VTEC in its standard engines. For 2016+ cars, the main ones are the 1.5L L15B turbo and the 2.0L K20C four-cylinder. The Type R runs a hotter i-VTEC tune with a turbo. Before 2006, some trims had the older VTEC, and some base engines had no VTEC at all. On any used Civic from 2006 onward, i-VTEC is standard.

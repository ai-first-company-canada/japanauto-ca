## What is a VIN?

A Vehicle Identification Number is the 17-character alphanumeric code carried by every road vehicle built for the North American market since the 1981 model year, in a format based on the ISO 3779 standard. Cars built for the Japanese home market use a shorter Japanese chassis number instead. Each code belongs to one vehicle and is never reused. Every record a car generates over its life is filed under it: registration, insurance, recalls, accident history, liens, and service work at any dealer or independent shop.

The 17 characters follow a set structure in three sections. Characters one to three are the World Manufacturer Identifier, which tells you who built the vehicle and where. A WMI starting with "J" means Japanese assembly. "1", "4" or "5" means the United States, and "2" means Canada. Characters four to nine describe the vehicle: model line, body style, engine, transmission, restraint system. Position nine is a check digit, calculated so that transcription errors show up. Characters ten to seventeen make up the serial portion. Position ten gives the model year (a letter or number on a 30-year rotating cycle), position eleven the assembly plant, and the last six the vehicle's own sequential build number.

VINs never use the letters I, O, or Q, which are too easily confused with 1 and 0. So a VIN has 17 characters but does not use all 26 letters.

## Why it matters in Canada

Every vehicle record in Canada is filed by VIN. [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) runs its accident and lien reports against it. ServiceOntario, SAAQ in Quebec, ICBC in British Columbia, and Service Alberta all index registration history by VIN. Since January 1, 2025, designated manufacturers must offer an online VIN lookup for recalls, and Transport Canada's own recall database lets you search by make, model and year. The Ontario [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/), which an Ontario private seller must legally provide, is generated from it. Nothing in the system works around it.

That is why VIN tampering sits behind most Canadian used-vehicle title fraud. On a Japanese vehicle, the dashboard VIN you can read through the windshield is one of three or four places the number appears. The others are the door-jamb sticker, the engine block, and the firewall. The engine number is separate and identifies the engine, not the car. Before any money changes hands, check that the chassis VIN locations match each other and the seller's registration. Walk away from a car whose dashboard plate looks freshly reseated, whose door-jamb sticker has been replaced, or whose engine VIN has been ground off.

## Common questions

### Where do I find the VIN on a Japanese car?

Start with the lower driver-side corner of the windshield, readable from outside through the glass. Next is the door-jamb sticker, which you see when you open the driver's door. Japanese-market and JDM vehicles often have a third stamping on the firewall in the engine bay, on a metal plate or straight onto the bulkhead. The engine block carries an engine number. That is technically separate from the chassis VIN and is used to match the engine, not to identify the vehicle. All three chassis VIN locations should match each other and the registration document.

### What does each character in a VIN mean?

Positions 1 through 3 identify the manufacturer and country of origin. Positions 4 through 8 describe the vehicle: model, body style, engine, transmission, and restraint system. Position 9 is a check digit, calculated from the other 16 characters, that catches transcription errors. Position 10 gives the model year on a rotating 30-year cycle of letters and digits. "M", for example, meant 2021 in the current cycle and will mean 2051 in the next. Position 11 is the assembly plant. Positions 12 through 17 are the sequential build number for that vehicle, within that plant and model year.

### How do I check a VIN for recalls in Canada?

Use the manufacturer's VIN recall lookup. Since January 1, 2025, Transport Canada has required designated vehicle manufacturers to offer one on their Canadian websites: enter the 17-character VIN and it lists the recalls that apply to that exact vehicle. Transport Canada's own Motor Vehicle Safety Recalls Database is free too, but it searches by make, model and year, so it shows every recall for the model rather than the status of one car. Safety recalls are normally repaired at no charge at a same-brand Canadian dealer, so ask the dealer to confirm an open recall before you buy. Recall information also shows up on [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) reports.

### Can a VIN tell me if a car is JDM?

The first character is the best clue, but only a clue. A "J" means the car was assembled in Japan. It does not say whether it was built for Japan or for export: a Lexus built in Japan for the Canadian market also starts with "J". A better test combines the WMI, the model code in positions 4 through 8, and a [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) report with no Canadian or US-spec dealer record. A genuine [JDM](/glossary/jdm/) import usually has no Canadian service history before its [RIV](/glossary/riv-program/) registration date and may have a Japanese-format chassis number on the firewall plate.

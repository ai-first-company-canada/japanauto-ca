## What is a catalytic converter?

A catalytic converter is an exhaust-system emissions device that uses precious-metal catalysts to chemically convert harmful tailpipe pollutants into less harmful gases. The catalysts, usually platinum, palladium and rhodium, coat a ceramic or metallic substrate inside a stainless-steel housing. In a modern "three-way" converter they drive three reactions at once. Carbon monoxide is oxidized to carbon dioxide. Unburnt hydrocarbons are oxidized to carbon dioxide and water. Nitrogen oxides are reduced to nitrogen and oxygen.

Catalytic converters have been mandatory on Canadian-market petrol vehicles since 1975, under federal emissions regulations modelled on US EPA standards. The technology has come a long way since the two-way catalysts of the 1970s, and a three-way catalyst on a Japanese vehicle today regularly works well past 200,000 kilometres. Failure usually shows up as a Check Engine Light set by the downstream oxygen sensor. The codes to look for are P0420 (catalyst efficiency below threshold, bank 1) and P0430 (same, bank 2).

Replacement is one of the most expensive emissions repairs on a typical Japanese vehicle. An [OEM](/glossary/oem/) Toyota or Honda catalyst can top $2,000 CAD installed at a dealer on V6 vehicles like the Camry V6, Pilot or RAV4. Aftermarket units from California Air Resources Board (CARB)-compliant suppliers cost roughly half that.

## Why it matters in Canada

Catalytic converter theft is a sustained crime epidemic in Canada. Police in Toronto, Montreal, Calgary and Vancouver all report year-over-year increases in thefts from parked vehicles. Metal prices drive it. One catalyst can hold $200 to $800 worth of platinum, palladium and rhodium, and thieves sell it to scrap-metal recyclers for cash on the spot. With a battery-powered reciprocating saw, cutting one out from under a car takes less than two minutes.

Among the Japanese brands we list, thieves go after the Toyota Prius (several generations) and the Honda Element most. On both, the catalyst is mounted where it is easy to reach, and it carries more precious metal than usual for the engine size. The Prius hybrid system runs the engine on and off, so the catalyst spends more time cold and needs extra precious metal to make up for it. That is a side effect of hybrid emissions design, and it makes Prius catalysts especially attractive to thieves.

Removing or disabling a catalyst breaks emissions-tampering rules. The exhaust is part of the [Provincial Safety Inspection](/glossary/provincial-safety-inspection/), which Ontario requires whenever a used vehicle changes owners, and a car missing its catalyst, whether stolen or removed in an aftermarket "delete", can fail it. British Columbia doesn't inspect cars on ordinary private transfers, but vehicles coming in from another province do get a full inspection there.

## Common questions

### Why are catalytic converters being stolen in Canada?

Because of the platinum, palladium and rhodium inside, which have traded at high prices through the 2020s. A single catalyst holds $200 to $800 of recoverable metal, and scrap-metal recyclers pay cash. Removal takes under two minutes with a battery-powered reciprocating saw, and a parked car offers no resistance. Police in Toronto, Montreal, Calgary and Vancouver all report steady year-over-year increases. Among Japanese vehicles, the Toyota Prius and Honda Element are hit most often because their catalysts carry more metal and sit in easy-to-reach spots.

### Can I drive a Toyota Prius without a catalytic converter?

The car will move, for a short distance. It is illegal on the road, it will fail inspection, and the engine management system drops into a degraded mode that can damage parts further down the line. A Prius (or any modern Japanese vehicle) without its catalyst will set Check Engine Light code P0420 and likely P0430. It can fail a [Provincial Safety Inspection](/glossary/provincial-safety-inspection/), such as the Ontario Safety Standards Certificate, and may fail emissions testing where that applies. Drive it straight to a repair shop and nowhere else. On a Prius, replacement usually runs $1,500 to $2,500 CAD with an OEM catalyst, less with a CARB-compliant aftermarket one.

### How much does a catalytic converter cost for a Honda?

An [OEM](/glossary/oem/) Honda catalytic converter costs $1,200 to $2,500 CAD installed at a dealer, depending on engine and emissions setup. The 3.5L J35 V6 in the Odyssey, Pilot and Acura MDX sits at the top of that range because the V6 uses two catalysts, one per cylinder bank. The 1.5L turbo and 2.0L K20C four-cylinders sit at the bottom. Aftermarket catalysts from CARB-compliant suppliers (the standard most provinces accept for emissions) usually cost $400 to $800 plus installation. Magnaflow, Walker and Eastern make direct-fit units that Canadian shops commonly use.

### Do JDM cars have catalytic converters?

Yes. [JDM](/glossary/jdm/) vehicles built for sale in Japan have always had catalytic converters, because Japanese emissions rules have required them since the 1970s. The catalyst setup may differ from Canadian-market specs, since Japanese standards split from North American ones on some details. A JDM car imported under the 15-year rule normally arrives with its Japanese-spec catalyst, though on an old car it may have been replaced at some point. Replacement JDM-spec catalysts may have to come from Japanese parts specialists such as Amayama or Megazip instead of a Canadian dealer's parts counter. Fifteen-year-old JDM imports don't go through the federal [RIV](/glossary/riv-program/) inspection, so have catalyst presence and condition checked at the provincial safety inspection or a pre-purchase inspection.

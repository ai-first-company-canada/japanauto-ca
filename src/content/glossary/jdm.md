## What is JDM?

JDM stands for Japanese Domestic Market. It describes vehicles, engines, transmissions and parts originally built for retail sale inside Japan rather than for export. A JDM Honda Civic Type R built for Tokyo showrooms in 2007 is a different car from the export-spec Civic Si sold in Calgary that year. The chassis tuning, emissions equipment and interior trim all differ, and the Japanese car is right-hand drive.

The famous performance cars are only part of it. Toyota Chaser, Nissan Skyline GT-R R32 through R34, Mitsubishi Lancer Evolution, Subaru Impreza WRX STi 22B: those get the headlines. The category also takes in plainer stuff, like Honda Acty kei trucks, the Nissan Pulsar GTI-R, Toyota Hiace vans in configurations never exported, and right-hand-drive versions of ordinary sedans that some Canadian buyers want for the novelty.

Many JDM cars still carry Japanese inspection markers, such as the Shaken sticker and the original Japanese plates. Neither is a legal document in Canada, and both usually come off during the import process. They do show you the car's documented history before it left Japan.

## Why it matters in Canada

Canada is one of the most permissive JDM import markets in North America. Transport Canada treats a vehicle more than 15 years old as not regulated at the time of importation, with age counted from the month and year of manufacture rather than the model year. In 2026 that opens the door to JDM cars built in 2011 or earlier, as long as the manufacture date can be proven to CBSA. The United States makes owners wait twenty-five years.

Most JDM cars arrive by ship from Japanese ports, and Pacific arrivals come through BC. Brokers then truck them to buyers across the country.

Because a 15-year-old JDM car isn't regulated under the Motor Vehicle Safety Act at import, it doesn't go through the [RIV Program](/glossary/riv-program/): no RIV fee, no Form 2 inspection, no federal modification list. You clear customs with CBSA (Form 1), pay GST and any duty, and then deal with the province. Alberta calls its check the Out-of-Province Inspection, done at a facility licensed under Alberta's Vehicle Inspection Program by a licensed journeyperson technician. Ontario requires a Safety Standards Certificate. Passing customs doesn't guarantee registration, and Transport Canada itself notes that some provinces won't license right-hand-drive vehicles.

## Common questions

### Can I import a JDM car to Canada?

Yes, if the vehicle is at least fifteen years old on the import date. Age counts from date of manufacture, not model year, and CBSA checks the proof of age at the border. Younger Japan-market vehicles generally can't be imported, because they weren't built to Canadian standards; Transport Canada lists only narrow exceptions. The usual route: a buyer or broker buys at a Japanese auction (USS, JU, TAA), ships by RoRo or container, clears customs with CBSA, and then registers the car with the province after its inspection. RIV isn't part of it.

### What is the 15-year rule for importing cars to Canada?

It is the federal age threshold past which a vehicle is no longer regulated under the Motor Vehicle Safety Act at the time of importation, so it can come in without meeting Canadian Motor Vehicle Safety Standards. Under fifteen years, only vehicles that comply with CMVSS can come in, which in practice means cars built for the Canadian or US market (US ones go through the [RIV Program](/glossary/riv-program/)). Past fifteen years, there are no federal modification requirements at import. Registration is still up to the province.

### What is the difference between a JDM and a Canadian-spec car?

A Canadian-spec car was built for sale here, met all CMVSS when it was made, and was probably sold new through a Canadian dealer. A JDM car was built for Japan. It is often right-hand drive and may have Japan-specific emissions hardware. Its odometer usually reads in kilometres from the factory (now standard worldwide, though older mile/km swaps turn up), and some of its safety equipment may fall short of current Canadian standards. Mechanically, many share engines and platforms with export models. Paperwork and parts cross-referencing take more work.

### Are JDM cars legal in all Canadian provinces?

Federally, a JDM car 15 years or older can be imported (it doesn't go through [RIV](/glossary/riv-program/)), but registration is provincial and the rules differ. Quebec is the strictest: the SAAQ won't let right-hand-drive vehicles built less than 25 years ago onto public roads, with a few exceptions, and it advises kei vehicles to stay off roads posted above 70 km/h. Rules elsewhere vary, so confirm with your provincial registry before you buy. Some insurers add a surcharge or refuse coverage on RHD vehicles. Before you buy, check your province's registration rules and your insurer's RHD policy.

## What is a UVIP?

A Used Vehicle Information Package (UVIP) is a document Ontario requires the seller to hand over in every private used-vehicle sale in the province. The requirement comes from the Ontario Highway Traffic Act and its regulations, administered by the Ministry of Transportation. ServiceOntario generates the package from the central registration database using the vehicle's [VIN](/glossary/vin/), and it currently costs $20.

What's inside is fixed. You get the vehicle and VIN details (year, make, model, body style, colour) and the vehicle's registration history in Ontario, meaning every registered owner on record. It also shows any lien registered against the vehicle in Ontario's Personal Property Security Registry and the previous retail sale price as last reported. Then there is the wholesale value the Ministry of Transportation will use to calculate Retail Sales Tax (RST) at registration. That figure matters most. Ontario charges 13 percent RST on private used-vehicle sales, based on whichever is higher: the actual sale price or the Canadian Black Book wholesale value. In effect, the UVIP sets the buyer's tax bill.

## Why it matters in Canada

No other province has a mandatory disclosure package like it. In Alberta, [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/) sets dealer disclosure obligations and there is the [Provincial Safety Inspection](/glossary/provincial-safety-inspection/) report, but nothing like a UVIP applies to private sales. British Columbia has ICBC Vehicle Claims History Reports and lien searches through the BC Personal Property Registry, each ordered separately rather than bundled. Quebec has SAAQ records and a separate RDPRM lien search, again with no single package.

If you're buying a used Japanese vehicle privately in Toronto or Ottawa, start with the UVIP. The seller can't legally withhold it, and ServiceOntario won't complete the registration without it. You can also order a UVIP yourself for any Ontario-registered vehicle before you start negotiating. At $20, that is often money well spent.

## Common questions

### How much does a UVIP cost in Ontario?

$20 from ServiceOntario, paid by whoever requests it (normally the seller, or a buyer ordering one independently). In a private sale, the seller must give the buyer the UVIP at no extra charge. You can order it online on the ServiceOntario website with the [VIN](/glossary/vin/) and licence plate, or in person at any ServiceOntario location. The province sets the fee and can change it, so check the current amount at ontario.ca/page/buy-used-vehicle.

### Is a UVIP the same as a CarFax?

No. The content overlaps, but they are different documents. The UVIP comes from the Ontario government and carries provincial registry data: Ontario registration history, liens, and the values used for sales tax. [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) is a private history report that pulls together accident records, insurance claims, liens across several provinces, US import/export markers, stolen-vehicle status, and recall information. For a used Japanese vehicle in Ontario, get both. They cost different amounts and draw on different data.

### Do I need a UVIP if I buy from a dealer?

No. The UVIP rule covers only private used-vehicle sales in Ontario. Dealer sales fall under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act, 2002 and [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) disclosure rules, which call for a different set of documents, including any registered liens, accident history the dealer knows about, and the dealer's licence number. Dealers don't have to provide a UVIP, though some Ontario dealers include one in their disclosure paperwork.

### Is there a UVIP equivalent in Alberta or BC?

Not formally. In Alberta, [AMVIC](/glossary/amvic/) disclosure rules make dealers provide accident history, lien status, and registration history, but private sellers face lighter obligations. The [Provincial Safety Inspection](/glossary/provincial-safety-inspection/) (called the Out-of-Province Inspection in Alberta) checks the vehicle mechanically and is not a registry document. In British Columbia you can order an ICBC Vehicle Claims History Report for accident history and a lien search through the BC Personal Property Registry, each separately by VIN. For a private sale anywhere outside Ontario, a [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) report plus a provincial lien search is usually the closest you'll get to a UVIP.

The Ontario rules described here were in force as of May 2026. For a specific deal, OMVIC or ServiceOntario can confirm what applies.

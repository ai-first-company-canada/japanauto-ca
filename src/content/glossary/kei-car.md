## What is a kei car?

A kei car, formally 軽自動車 (kei-jidousha, "light automobile"), is a class of Japanese vehicle defined by tax and registration rules, not by performance or styling. Current Japanese rules cap kei vehicles at 3.4 m long, 1.48 m wide, 2.0 m high and 660 cc of engine displacement. A vehicle inside those limits pays less Japanese road tax, gets parking exemptions in some Japanese cities, and wears the yellow licence plate that sets it apart from regular passenger cars.

Japan created the category in 1949 to speed up post-war motorisation, giving working-class families small, cheap vehicles they could afford. The first limits were tighter (150 cc engines and shorter bodies) and have been loosened over the decades. The 660 cc limit dates from 1990, and the current body dimensions from 1998. Kei vehicles come in a surprising spread of body styles. There are small passenger cars (Honda N-Box, Suzuki Wagon R, Daihatsu Move), kei trucks (Honda Acty, Suzuki Carry, Daihatsu Hijet), kei vans, and even sports cars like the Honda Beat, Honda S660 and Mazda Autozam AZ-1.

japanauto.ca covers nine [Japanese brands](/glossary/jdm/): Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Mazda, Subaru, Lexus, Acura, Infiniti and Mitsubishi. Among those, the kei vehicles that matter to Canadian buyers are mostly Honda's. The Acty kei truck was discontinued in 2021, and Actys built 15 or more years ago can be imported. Honda's 1990s Beat sports kei is old enough already. The S660 went on sale in 2015, so the earliest ones won't reach 15 years until 2030.

## Why it matters in Canada

Kei vehicles come in under the same 15-year rule as other [JDM](/glossary/jdm/) cars. Once a kei truck or sports kei is fifteen years past its date of manufacture, Transport Canada no longer regulates it at import, so it clears customs with CBSA without going through the [RIV Program](/glossary/riv-program/) or a federal modification list. You need proof of the manufacture date.

Registration is where provinces differ, and clearing customs doesn't guarantee a plate. Quebec is the clearest case: the SAAQ won't let right-hand-drive vehicles built less than 25 years ago onto public roads (with a few exceptions), and it advises drivers of kei vehicles to avoid roads with a posted limit over 70 km/h. Other provinces have their own rules and inspections, so check with your provincial registry before you buy. Farms, ski resorts, golf courses and small businesses buy kei trucks because a compact truck that cheap makes commercial sense.

In practice, insurance matters as much as registration. Even where kei vehicles are road-legal, some insurers won't cover a kei truck as a daily driver, or add surcharges that wipe out the cost advantage. Check that the vehicle can be registered and insured before you import it.

## Common questions

### Can I drive a kei truck in Canada?

It depends on the province. A kei truck that is 15+ years old can be imported without going through the [RIV Program](/glossary/riv-program/), but road use needs provincial registration, usually after a provincial inspection. Quebec won't register right-hand-drive vehicles under 25 years old for public roads and tells kei drivers to stay off roads posted above 70 km/h. Other provinces set their own conditions, so ask your provincial registry before you import. Insurance varies by carrier, so confirm coverage before you buy too. Use on private property such as farms and golf courses doesn't need road registration.

### What does kei mean in Japanese?

Kei (軽) means "light" or "lightweight". The full term, kei-jidousha (軽自動車), translates to "light automobile" and names the Japanese tax and registration category for small vehicles within set dimension and engine-size limits. "Light" here is a tax and registration class. The cars do end up light, since the size limits leave little room for weight.

### Is a kei car the same as a JDM car?

Kei is a subset of [JDM](/glossary/jdm/). Every kei vehicle is JDM by definition: it is built for sale in Japan under Japan's own tax and registration rules, and the category wouldn't exist outside that system. Plenty of JDM vehicles are not kei. The Honda Civic Type R, Nissan Skyline GT-R, Toyota Chaser and most mainstream Japan-market sedans and crossovers are too big or have engines over the kei limits.

### Are kei trucks legal in Ontario?

We couldn't confirm a single published Ontario rule that covers every kei truck, so treat it as a question to settle before you buy. Road use needs Ontario registration through ServiceOntario and a Safety Standards Certificate, and some insurers refuse to cover right-hand-drive vehicles as daily drivers even when they can be registered. Use on private property (farms, golf courses, ski resorts) doesn't need road registration. If you're in Ontario and want a kei truck for road use, confirm with the Ministry of Transportation that it can be registered, and get an insurance quote, before you import.

## What is a salvage title?

A salvage title is a registration status given to a vehicle that an insurer has declared a total loss. The trigger is money. When repairs would cost more than the vehicle is worth to the insurer, the insurer pays the claim, takes ownership of the wreck, and re-registers it under a salvage brand that flags the title permanently.

Each province runs its own brand categories. Ontario has four: None (no brand), Salvage (written off, can be repaired or used for parts, not driveable), Rebuilt (formerly Salvage, repaired, passed a structural inspection, now driveable), and Irreparable (parts or scrap only, never driveable again in Ontario). Other provinces use their own labels, and several call the parts-only category Non-repairable. In British Columbia branding is handled by ICBC, Alberta applies its own brands through its registry system, and Quebec uses SAAQ designations under the Highway Safety Code.

The brand stays with the [VIN](/glossary/vin/). Once a vehicle is branded Salvage, that history never leaves the registration record, even after it is rebuilt and re-inspected to Rebuilt status. Rebuilt tells you the car was fixed and passed inspection, and it still discloses the salvage past. You will see the brand on every [CarFax Canada](/glossary/carfax/) report and on provincial documents, including the Ontario [UVIP](/glossary/uvip/).

## Why it matters in Canada

A Salvage or Rebuilt brand hits two things: insurance and financing. Many Canadian insurers will write only third-party liability on a Rebuilt vehicle and refuse comprehensive and collision outright. Some won't write a policy at all, especially when the original damage was structural rather than cosmetic. Most prime auto lenders will not finance a Rebuilt vehicle. Subprime money is sometimes available, at noticeably higher rates than for the same car with a clean title.

The result is steep depreciation. A 2018 RAV4 that went from Salvage to Rebuilt usually lists at 50 to 65 percent of the price of an identical clean-title RAV4. That discount is the trade-off for the insurance and financing limits. Some buyers like the math, typically cash buyers who self-insure or are fine with liability-only coverage. Most walk away.

[JDM](/glossary/jdm/) imports have a gap of their own. A vehicle brought in from Japan under the 15-year rule may carry Japanese auction-house damage records (USS, JU, TAA grading) showing serious prior damage. If it was never insurance-totalled in Canada, because the damage happened in Japan before import, it can be sold here without any "salvage" brand despite a real repair history. So on any JDM import, get a pre-purchase inspection and a paint-thickness gauge check, even when the CarFax is clean.

## Common questions

### Can I drive a salvage-titled car in Ontario?

No. A Salvage-branded vehicle cannot legally be driven on Ontario roads. It first has to be repaired and pass a structural inspection at a DriveON Vehicle Inspection Centre. The owner then takes the Structural Inspection Certificate, with photos of the damage and invoices for the major parts used, to ServiceOntario to have the brand changed to Rebuilt. A Safety Standards Certificate is also needed before the rebuilt car can be plated for the road. Driving a Salvage vehicle on Ontario roads is a Highway Traffic Act offence, and the insurance on it is invalid.

### What is the difference between salvage and rebuilt?

Salvage goes on the moment an insurer declares a total loss: the car is written off, the insurer owns it, and it is not currently driveable. Rebuilt comes later, once the vehicle has been repaired and passed a structural inspection to ministry standards, and the Structural Inspection Certificate has been filed with ServiceOntario. You can legally drive a Rebuilt vehicle. You cannot drive a Salvage one. Both brands stay with the [VIN](/glossary/vin/) and disclose the total-loss history. The path runs Clean → Salvage → Rebuilt and never back to Clean.

### Will insurance cover a rebuilt car in Canada?

Often only partly. Many Canadian insurers will write third-party liability on a Rebuilt vehicle and decline comprehensive and collision, and some refuse coverage altogether. It is hardest when the original damage was structural (frame damage, airbag deployment, water immersion) rather than cosmetic. Specialty insurers that cover Rebuilt vehicles exist, but their premiums run well above clean-title rates. Check availability and price with your insurer before you buy. Insurance is the biggest single reason Rebuilt vehicles sell at 50 to 65 percent of clean-title comparables.

### Do JDM imports get a salvage title?

Sometimes. A [JDM](/glossary/jdm/) vehicle imported under the 15-year rule that is insurance-totalled in Canada after import gets the usual provincial salvage brand. One badly damaged in Japan before import, which is what auction grades R and RA flag, can be sold here with no Canadian salvage brand, because no Canadian insurer ever recorded the damage. That is a blind spot in how Canada brands imported vehicles. For any JDM import, a pre-purchase inspection and a paint-thickness gauge check on the body panels are a must, whatever the CarFax says.

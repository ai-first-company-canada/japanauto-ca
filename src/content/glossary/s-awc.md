## What is S-AWC?

S-AWC stands for Super All Wheel Control. It is Mitsubishi's integrated vehicle-dynamics system, first built for the 2007 Lancer Evolution X performance sedan and later adapted for crossover duty on the Canadian-market Outlander and Outlander PHEV. What sets it apart from a conventional AWD setup is that one electronic controller runs everything: AWD torque distribution, Active Yaw Control (rear-axle torque vectoring), Active Stability Control, and [ABS](/glossary/abs/). Most AWD vehicles treat those as separate systems.

The gasoline Outlander uses front-wheel brake-based torque vectoring and an electronically controlled centre coupling. The coupling shifts torque between the front and rear axles. In a corner, the system brakes the inside front wheel to mimic torque vectoring, and it works with stability control to manage yaw during an evasive manoeuvre.

The Outlander [PHEV](/glossary/phev/) is built differently, and it is the more capable of the two. Twin electric motors, one per axle, drive the front and rear wheels independently, while a 2.4L Atkinson-cycle gasoline engine mostly acts as a generator. Because torque is delivered electronically, there is no mechanical clutch to engage, so torque allocation happens in milliseconds.

## Why it matters in Canada

Mitsubishi Canada's 10-year / 160,000 km powertrain warranty is the longest among mainstream Japanese brands sold here, and it covers the S-AWC drivetrain components. For a used buyer that matters, because the coverage runs from the original in-service date and the balance can pass to later owners, for example through Mitsubishi's certified pre-owned program. Conditions apply, so have a Mitsubishi dealer confirm what is left on the specific VIN.

Winter is where the PHEV version earns its keep. A conventional AWD system has to wait for sensors to detect slip and then engage a mechanical clutch. That takes milliseconds, but you can still feel the lag on some ice-to-asphalt transitions. The twin-motor PHEV skips the mechanical step entirely. Torque moves between axles electronically and instantly, and grip recovery on Canadian winter pavement is measurably better as a result.

## Common questions

### What does S-AWC stand for?

Super All Wheel Control. Mitsubishi uses the name for its integrated vehicle-dynamics system, which ties AWD torque distribution, Active Yaw Control (rear-axle torque vectoring on certain implementations), Active Stability Control, and [ABS](/glossary/abs/) into one electronic architecture. It started on the 2007 Lancer Evolution X, a high-performance sedan, and now does crossover duty on the Canadian-market Outlander and Outlander PHEV.

### Is S-AWC the same on the Outlander PHEV and the regular Outlander?

No. The gasoline Outlander pairs front-wheel brake-based torque vectoring with an electronically controlled centre coupling, a clever reactive setup that is still mechanically conventional. The Outlander [PHEV](/glossary/phev/) uses twin electric motors (one per axle), so the front and rear drivelines are mechanically decoupled. With a separate motor on each axle there is no clutch to engage, and torque can be reallocated in milliseconds. On ice, the PHEV version is the more capable of the two.

### Does Mitsubishi's 10-year warranty cover S-AWC?

Yes. Mitsubishi Canada's 10-year / 160,000 km powertrain warranty covers the engine, the transmission, and the S-AWC drivetrain components. The term counts from the vehicle's original in-service date, so a used buyer gets whatever balance is left, and only if the transfer conditions are met (Mitsubishi's certified pre-owned cars come with the balance). Confirm the warranty status for the specific VIN with Mitsubishi Canada or a Mitsubishi dealer before you buy.

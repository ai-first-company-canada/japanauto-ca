## What is AMVIC?

AMVIC stands for the Alberta Motor Vehicle Industry Council. It is the delegated administrative authority that licenses and supervises every automotive business in the province under the Consumer Protection Act and the Automotive Business Regulation. That covers new- and used-car dealers, wholesalers and brokers, leasing and consignment businesses, repair shops, and the salespeople who work for licensed dealers.

Alberta handed the job to a not-for-profit, industry-funded body that works at arm's length from the provincial government, the same model Ontario uses with [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) and British Columbia with the [VSA](/glossary/vsa/). AMVIC was established in 1999. It sets licensing standards, investigates complaints and pursues curbsiders, under oversight from the Alberta government.

Business licences come in four classes: automotive sales (retailers, wholesalers, agents and brokers), leasing, consignment and repair. Salespeople are registered separately and must work for a licensed business. A dealership with a service bay typically holds both a sales and a repair licence.

## Why it matters in Canada

Any dealer in Alberta selling you a used Japanese vehicle, whether in Calgary, Edmonton, Red Deer, Lethbridge or Grande Prairie, must hold an active AMVIC licence. The licence number has to appear on every advertisement, online listings on japanauto.ca included. Checking takes thirty seconds. Type the business name into the "Find a Licensed Business" search on amvic.org and you will see active status, expiry date and any disciplinary history.

For used Japanese cars this matters in two specific ways. JDM imports and out-of-province purchases are where curbsiding (selling vehicles for profit without a licence) most often starts, and convictions can bring fines, vehicle forfeiture and, rarely, jail time. And AMVIC's industry-funded compensation fund can pay back buyers who lose money through a licensed dealer's misconduct, up to a per-claim maximum the AMVIC Board sets. A private sale gets neither protection. On a private deal, get a pre-purchase inspection every time.

## Common questions

### Who needs an AMVIC licence in Alberta?

If you sell, advertise or broker retail vehicle sales in Alberta as a business, you need an AMVIC dealer licence. Anyone employed to negotiate or close those sales needs a salesperson licence. The test is intent to profit. Selling your own car privately needs no licence. Selling more than a small handful a year, even off a residential driveway, generally does. Repair businesses need their own licence class. Inspectors doing the Alberta Out-of-Province Inspection are licensed separately under Alberta's Vehicle Inspection Program, not by AMVIC.

### How do I check if an Alberta car dealer is AMVIC-licensed?

Use the licensee search on amvic.org. Search by legal business name, trade name, or the AMVIC licence number printed in the dealer's ad. You get the licence type, current status (active, expired, suspended or cancelled), expiry date, registered address and any public disciplinary action from the past several years. If the dealer has no number, or the number doesn't match an active record, walk away.

### What does the AMVIC consumer compensation fund cover?

It pays Alberta consumers back for provable financial losses caused by an AMVIC-licensed business. Typical cases are undisclosed liens, a vehicle never delivered after a deposit, or misrepresentation that the AMVIC board upholds after investigating. Each claim is capped at a figure the board sets. Private sales, curbsider sales and losses where the buyer skipped AMVIC's dispute-resolution channels are excluded. To file, you need proof of the transaction, proof of the loss, and proof that you first tried to settle it with the dealer.

### Is AMVIC the same as OMVIC?

No. AMVIC covers Alberta. [OMVIC](/glossary/omvic/) covers Ontario under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Act, 2002, and British Columbia has the [Vehicle Sales Authority](/glossary/vsa/) under the Motor Dealer Act. They cooperate informally, but each issues its own licences, runs its own compensation fund and sets its own fees and disclosure rules. A dealer licensed in one province has no standing in another until it gets a licence there.

Current to Alberta rules as of May 2026. If a specific deal is on the line, call AMVIC directly or talk to a lawyer.

## Why Subaru dominates Canadian winters

Take a typical Calgary switch: an FWD Civic on all-seasons traded for a 2018 Outback at around $24,500 after one chinook freeze-thaw too many. A year later that Outback would usually still be worth close to what was paid. In Western Canada, a used Subaru barely depreciates once it reaches a certain age.

The reason is the drivetrain. Subaru has been selling four-wheel-drive cars since the 1970s, and there's real engineering behind its [Symmetrical](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) full-time AWD slogan. Most rivals drive the rear axle only after a sensor notices slip; a Subaru, with its boxer engine mounted lengthwise, drives all four wheels all the time. In wet snow there's no delay to wait through. Winter tires are still mandatory in our book. On a set of Blizzaks, an Outback is about as close to an all-conditions car as the price bracket allows.

Subaru sells AWD as standard on every model in Canada except the rear-wheel-drive BRZ sports coupe. No other mainstream Japanese brand does that, and it's the main reason Subaru sells well above its national share in the snowiest parts of the country (Quebec, Atlantic Canada, Northern Ontario).

Out West, a used Subaru usually means an Outback. The WRX sells to its own crowd of enthusiasts. The three-row Ascent arrived in 2019 and sells to families in suburban GTA and Calgary.

## Symmetrical AWD explained

Subaru's [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) is laid out differently from any other AWD system sold in Canada. The boxer engine, with pistons moving horizontally instead of vertically, sits lengthwise, low in the chassis and on the car's centreline. The transmission, transfer case and driveshafts line up along that same centreline, hence the name. Torque distribution to all four wheels is continuous rather than reactive, with active torque vectoring on most modern trims through the [Lineartronic CVT](/glossary/cvt/) and electronically managed centre coupling.

On a Canadian winter road, that means the AWD never has to "decide" to engage. It's always on. When you accelerate from a stop on packed snow at the top of Granville Street in Vancouver or on a Calgary residential road in February, the front and rear wheels are receiving torque simultaneously and proportionally. Most AWD systems on Toyota, Honda, Nissan, and Mazda crossovers run mainly in front-wheel drive and bring in the rear axle when slip is detected or predicted, within milliseconds. You can't feel that delay, but testing can measure it.

## Subaru reliability and head gasket history

The 2.5L FB25 boxer engine fitted to the modern Outback, Forester and Legacy goes 250,000+ km with regular oil changes, and the Ascent uses a 2.4L turbo (FA24) only. Subaru's old reputation problem, head gaskets on the EJ-series boxers, belongs to roughly 1999-2010 cars. It doesn't show up on the FB-series engines, which arrived in the Forester for 2011, the Impreza for 2012 and the Outback and Legacy for 2013.

The newer issue to watch is oil consumption on early FB engines, which Subaru addressed through extended warranties on affected vehicles (in the US class action: 2011-2014 Forester, 2013 Outback and Legacy, 2012-2013 Impreza and 2013 Crosstrek). Verify oil consumption history on any car from those years before purchase. The [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) Lineartronic transmission is durable in Canadian use; treat fluid changes at the manufacturer-specified interval as non-negotiable on a used Subaru with over 100,000 km.

## Common questions

### Is Subaru really better in winter than other AWD cars?

When grip keeps changing (packed snow broken by slush, deep accumulation, ice patches on wet asphalt), yes. [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) has a measurable advantage over reactive AWD systems on Toyota, Honda, Nissan, and Mazda crossovers. On plowed city commutes the gap is small enough that tires matter more than the AWD hardware. In Quebec, Atlantic Canada, Northern Ontario or rural BC, the Subaru earns its reputation. In downtown Toronto or Vancouver, call it a tie with Mazda or Toyota.

### Which used Subaru is best for Canadian winters?

Our pick is a 2019-2022 Outback 2.5i Touring. It has 8.7 inches of ground clearance, more than most compact SUVs (RAV4 8.4, CX-5 7.6), room for a family's gear, and resale that holds. With good winter tires, its [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) handles anything a Canadian winter throws at it short of real off-roading. For tighter budgets, a 2018-2020 Forester is roughly $4,000 cheaper at the same age and kilometres and offers similar capability in a smaller package.

### Do Subaru head gasket problems still exist?

Not on modern engines. The head gasket failures belong to the older EJ-series boxer engines fitted to Subarus from roughly 1999 through 2010. The current FB-series boxer engine, phased in from 2011 (Forester) to 2013 (Outback and Legacy), doesn't have that weakness, and it has no major failure mode on file with Transport Canada. A regularly serviced FB-engined Subaru isn't at risk.

### Is a used Subaru Outback better than a Forester?

Depends where you live. The Outback is bigger and more comfortable on long highway drives, with more cargo room. The Forester is shorter and easier to park downtown. Engine, transmission and AWD are the same. In the BC interior, the Alberta foothills or rural Quebec, the Outback's extra clearance and space are worth paying for. In Toronto, Vancouver or Montreal, buy the Forester. Pricing on a 2020 Outback Touring runs roughly $4,000 above an equivalent 2020 Forester Touring.

### How long does a Subaru engine last?

A well-maintained modern FB-series boxer engine routinely exceeds 250,000 kilometres in Canadian service, and many examples reach 350,000+ km with regular oil changes and timing-component service at recommended intervals. Older EJ-series engines were prone to head gasket failure at higher mileage. On an early FB engine (roughly 2011-2014 production), check the service records for oil consumption before you buy.

### Subaru or Mazda AWD for Canadian winter?

If your roads are rough and unpredictable, Subaru. Its always-on [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) is the more capable system. On plowed city roads Mazda's [i-Activ AWD](/glossary/awd/) is close enough that your tires matter more than the badge. After that it comes down to taste: the Mazda feels more polished inside, the Subaru more like a tool.

### Is a Subaru Crosstrek good for snow?

Yes. The Crosstrek shares the [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) architecture with the Outback and Forester and has 8.7 inches of ground clearance, more than most compact crossovers. The 2.0L FB20 engine in entry trims is less powerful than the Outback's 2.5L but adequate for the Crosstrek's lighter weight. Used 2018-2022 Crosstreks in Vancouver and the BC interior typically list between $22,000 and $30,000, and they sell quickly once the snow starts.

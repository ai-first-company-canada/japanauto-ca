## Why Hondas are popular in Canada

The Honda Civic has been the best-selling passenger car in Canada for more than 25 consecutive years. You can check it against Statistics Canada registration data and Honda Canada's annual reports. The streak began in 1998 and survived five redesigns, two recessions and the crossover boom, plus every Korean and German compact sent to knock it off.

Part of the explanation is where the car is built. Honda's Alliston plant in Ontario can build more than 400,000 Civics and CR-Vs a year for the North American market. Building here keeps dealer supply steady and parts moving quickly out of the Ontario distribution centre. Warranty work on Alliston-built cars also skips the cross-border delays some imported brands deal with. There are more than 230 Honda dealerships across the country, second only to Toyota among Japanese brands.

## Most popular used Honda models

In every Tier-1 city, Civics outnumber every other used Honda. A 2018 to 2021 Civic LX or EX in clean condition with 60,000 to 110,000 km lists between $19,000 and $26,000 in Toronto, Vancouver, and the GTA suburbs. Sport and Touring trims command an extra $1,500 to $2,500. The 2016-2021 generation with the 1.5L turbo earns the highest used-resale of any Civic generation since the 2006-2011 redesign. The turbo engine helps, and so does the manual gearbox, which Honda offered on fewer trims from 2022.

Walk any Toronto-suburb parking lot and count the CR-Vs. The 2017-2022 generation, especially the Touring with full Honda Sensing, sells steadily used across Markham, Oakville, Brampton, and the equivalent Western Canadian suburbs. The Accord (especially the Sport 2.0T trim) holds value in the GTA and Montreal. Families who need seven seats tend to land on the Pilot. Odyssey volume is shrinking, but it still sells in the suburbs, where the flexible seating earns its keep.

## Honda Civic: Canada's bestselling car

Twenty-five years covers at least two generations of Canadian buyers, and no single feature explains that. The 1.5L turbo (2016 onward, replacing the older 1.8L naturally aspirated) delivers stronger fuel economy than the previous generation while preserving the lightweight chassis dynamics. Mid-trim interiors are nicer than the price suggests. The [i-VTEC](/glossary/i-vtec/) variable valve system, layered over the 2.0L K20C turbo in the Type R, gives a measurable performance advantage at high RPM that no Korean or American competitor matches in the segment.

The duller reason matters more. Honda Canada has rarely raised the entry price faster than wages: a 2024 Civic LX still starts under $30,000, and a 2018 Civic LX with 100,000 km lists below $19,000.

## Honda reliability and longevity

Honda mostly deserves its engine reputation. The 1.5L L15 turbo has one known weak spot. On 2016-2018 Civics and 2017-2018 CR-Vs, fuel could dilute the oil on short trips in deep cold, and Honda Canada answered with a software update and an extended powertrain warranty, so ask whether that work was done. The 2.0L K20C [i-VTEC](/glossary/i-vtec/) turbo (Civic Type R, Accord 2.0T) comes from the engine family that has anchored Honda performance for two decades. The 3.5L J35 V6 in the Pilot, Odyssey, Ridgeline, and Acura MDX is the most-driven Honda powertrain in North America and routinely exceeds 350,000 km in service.

The known caveat is the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) transmission. Honda CVTs in mid-2010s Civic and CR-V applications had documented service bulletins for shudder under part-throttle and judder cold-shifts. The current CVT is much improved and the problem is mostly history for cars built after roughly 2018. On a 2014 to 2017 Civic or CR-V, ask for the CVT fluid records specifically.

## Common questions

### Is Honda reliable in Canada?

Yes. The 1.5L L15 turbo, 2.0L K20C [i-VTEC](/glossary/i-vtec/) and 3.5L J35 V6 are some of the toughest Japanese engines sold here, and Transport Canada's defects database lists no major failure pattern for any of them on late-2010s and newer cars. There are two known caveats. Early 1.5L turbos (2016-2018 Civic, 2017-2018 CR-V) could dilute their oil with fuel in short-trip winter driving, which Honda Canada addressed with a software update and extended warranty. And the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) on mid-2010s Civic and CR-V models had service bulletins for shudder and judder, addressed through software updates and fluid revisions. Always verify CVT service history on a used Civic or CR-V from 2014 to 2017.

### Why is the Honda Civic so popular in Canada?

Mostly price. The Civic has been Canada's best-selling passenger car for more than 25 consecutive years thanks to price discipline (entry trims still below $30,000 new in 2024), Ontario domestic manufacturing at the Alliston plant, durable powertrains across multiple generations, and Honda Canada's largest-in-class dealer service network for non-Toyota Japanese brands. It is also one of the few compacts in the Canadian market that still offered manual transmission through the 2021 model year, which preserves used demand among enthusiasts.

### How many kilometres can a Honda CR-V last?

A well-maintained Honda CR-V routinely exceeds 300,000 kilometres in Canadian service. The 2.4L K24 four-cylinder (pre-2017) and the 1.5L L15 turbo (2017+) are both capable of long service life with regular oil changes. The constraint on used CR-V longevity is the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) transmission on 2014 to 2017 examples, where fluid maintenance discipline matters more than the odometer reading. A 2018 or newer CR-V should go 250,000+ km on standard service.

### Are Honda CVT transmissions a problem?

On 2014 to 2017 Civic and CR-V applications, Honda CVTs had documented service bulletins for shudder under part-throttle and cold-shift judder. Honda issued software updates and fluid revisions to address them, and most affected vehicles were repaired under warranty when the issues surfaced. Post-2018 CVTs are much better and rarely show the problem. For any used Civic or CR-V in the 2014-2017 range, request a fluid-change history before purchase. For 2018 and newer, treat the CVT as a normal-service item with fluid changes at the manufacturer-specified interval.

### Is a used Honda Accord a good buy in Canada?

Yes, particularly the 2018-2022 generation Sport 2.0T which pairs the [i-VTEC](/glossary/i-vtec/)-equipped K20C engine with a 10-speed automatic. Resale on the Accord has been weaker than the Civic and CR-V in recent years, so used ones cost less than a comparable Camry. Typical 2019 Accord Sport 2.0T listings in Toronto run $24,000 to $28,000 against $26,000 to $31,000 for the comparable Camry. For the money, it's the better buy.

### Which year of Honda Civic is most reliable?

The 2018 to 2021 generation, particularly the Civic Sport and Si trims with the manual transmission, are the most reliable used Civics currently in the Canadian market. The 1.5L turbo is mature by that production window, the oil-dilution fix was in place and the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) issues from earlier in the generation were largely addressed. The 2006-2011 eighth-generation Civic with the 1.8L R18 is also exceptionally durable, but availability of clean examples is now limited.

### Honda or Toyota: which is better for Canadian winters?

Neither. In matching trims they behave the same in snow. AWD is offered on the Honda CR-V and Pilot and on the Toyota RAV4 and Highlander; FWD on Civic and Corolla. The single largest factor in Canadian winter safety is winter tires regardless of brand, and AWD does not shorten braking distance on either platform. Toyota offers a hybrid on more models; Honda's Ontario plant gives it slightly quicker parts supply. Spend the difference on good winter tires.

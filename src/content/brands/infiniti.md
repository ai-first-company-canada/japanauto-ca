## Why Infiniti is a luxury value play in Canada

Infiniti has about 40 retailers in Canada, mostly in the big metros, far fewer than Lexus or Acura. So you'll find fewer Infiniti listings on japanauto.ca, and the cars lose value faster than a Lexus or Acura of the same age. In Toronto and Vancouver, a 2020 QX60 lists $4,000 to $7,000 below a comparable 2020 Lexus RX 350. That discount is what you get paid for accepting a thinner dealer network and weaker resale.

If you plan to keep the car, that trade can pay off. The QX60 shares its platform and most of its hardware with the Nissan Pathfinder, so any of Nissan Canada's 200+ dealerships can service one. The 3.5L VQ35 V6, used in most Infinitis from the early 2000s to the late 2010s, made Ward's 10 Best Engines list more than a dozen times. Sharing with Nissan also keeps parts well below Lexus or BMW prices over the long haul.

## Most popular used Infiniti models

Families shopping used Infiniti mostly end up looking at QX60s. A 2019-2022 QX60 LUXE AWD with 60,000 to 110,000 km in Toronto, Vancouver, or Calgary lists between $30,000 and $42,000, roughly $5,000 below a comparable Acura MDX or Lexus RX. For 2022 the QX60 dropped its CVT for a 9-speed automatic and got a 295-hp 3.5L V6. It drives much better for it.

The QX50 (mid-size crossover) and the QX80 (full-size SUV) round out the SUV lineup. The Q50 sedan goes up against the BMW 3 Series and Audi A4 on the used market. The Red Sport 400, with its 3.0L twin-turbo V6, is the interesting one, and it loses value fast, which is good news if you're buying. The Q60 coupe sells mostly to enthusiasts.

The previous-generation Q70 sedan and FX/QX70 crossover are getting old, and buyers overlook them. Clean examples often list under $20,000 with 100,000 km on the VQ35 V6, which is a lot of luxury car for the money.

## Infiniti AWD for Canadian winter

Infiniti's AWD system on the QX60, QX50, QX80, and Q50/Q60 sport sedans is reactive. The front-drive-based QX60 and 2019-and-newer QX50 run mostly in front-wheel drive, the rear-drive-based Q50, Q60 and QX80 mostly in rear-wheel drive, and the other axle joins in when sensors detect slip. The QX60's system is shared with the Nissan Pathfinder and Murano. On plowed city streets it does about as well as any other reactive system. With proper winter tires it gets you off a packed-snow stop without fuss. When grip keeps changing, it falls well behind Subaru's full-time [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) or Acura's torque-vectoring [SH-AWD](/glossary/awd/).

The Q60 Red Sport 400 is offered in both AWD and RWD configurations. Buyers in Calgary, Edmonton, or Quebec winter conditions should buy the AWD version. A rear-drive luxury coupe on winter tires still makes every January commute harder than it needs to be.

## Infiniti vs Lexus and Acura

Lexus sells refinement and has the best J.D. Power dependability numbers. [Acura](/brands/acura/) sells handling, through [SH-AWD](/glossary/awd/) and cheap Honda-shared parts. Infiniti is the discount option of the three. It depreciates hardest, its parts are cheap through Nissan dealers, and its VQ V6 has the best engine pedigree of the group.

Keeping the car five to seven years? Infiniti usually costs the least overall. Trading in after three? Buy the Lexus, because its resale covers the higher price. Acura is the pick if driving feel and AWD matter most to you.

## Infiniti parts and Nissan-shared service

Under the skin, an Infiniti is mostly Nissan, and that's the money-saver. Any of Nissan Canada's 200+ dealerships can service a QX60, since its engine, transmission, suspension and most other systems are Pathfinder hardware. The 3.5L VQ35 V6 used across most of the Infiniti range is the same engine architecture as the Nissan 350Z, 370Z, Maxima, and Pathfinder. At the Infiniti counter, OEM parts cost about what Lexus charges. Denso, Aisin, NGK and KYB versions run 40 to 60 percent of dealer price at PartsAvatar.ca or PartsEngine.ca (background in the [OEM glossary](/glossary/oem/)).

## Common questions

### Is Infiniti reliable in Canada?

Mostly, yes. Which engine and gearbox the car has matters more than the badge. The 3.5L VQ35 V6 in most pre-2022 Infinitis has billions of kilometres behind it across Nissan and Infiniti models and ranks among the most durable V6s sold in North America. The 3.7L VQ37 in Q50, Q60, and Q70 trims has the same durability profile. The 2.0L VC-Turbo in the 2019-and-newer QX50 is mechanically more complex and the long-term reliability data is still building. The weak spot is the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) in some QX60s. It's the same JATCO unit as the Nissan Pathfinder's, with the same 2013-2018 durability problems. The 2022+ redesign moved away from CVT.

### Is a used Infiniti QX60 a good buy?

A 2022 or newer one, yes. That redesign replaced the CVT with a conventional 9-speed automatic, and the QX60 is much better for it. A 2022-2023 QX60 LUXE AWD with 30,000 to 70,000 km in Toronto or Vancouver lists between $42,000 and $52,000, well below a comparable Acura MDX or Lexus RX. On a 2013-2021 car, the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) is the risk, and you should walk away without verified service history. A 2020 QX60 with under-documented CVT history is not the value play it appears to be.

### Infiniti vs Lexus: which is better in Canada?

Lexus is the better car on refinement, dependability and resale. Infiniti is the better deal: it costs less used and Nissan dealers can fix it. Over five years or more, Infiniti usually costs less to own. If you'll sell in three years, Lexus resale makes it the safer buy. They also drive differently. The Q50 and Q60 are tuned firmer and sportier, while a Lexus leans toward comfort.

### Is Infiniti AWD good for Canadian winter?

For plowed city roads, yes. Infiniti's reactive AWD on QX60, QX50, QX80, and the Q50/Q60 AWD trims does about as well as most luxury rivals. It can't match Subaru's full-time [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) or Acura's torque-vectoring [SH-AWD](/glossary/awd/) for variable-grip conditions, but on proper winter tires it handles Toronto, Vancouver or Calgary streets without trouble. Avoid RWD-only Q50/Q60 trims for daily Canadian winter use regardless of how good the winter tires are.

### How long does an Infiniti VQ engine last?

A well-maintained 3.5L VQ35 V6 (the 2014-2020 QX60 and older Infinitis such as the G35, FX35 and M35, plus the Nissan 350Z) routinely exceeds 300,000 kilometres in Canadian service, with many examples reaching 400,000+ km on regular oil changes. The 3.7L VQ37 in the early Q50, Q60, Q70 and FX37/QX70 has the same durability profile. The VQ family has made Ward's 10 Best Engines list more than a dozen times across several generations.

### Are Infiniti parts expensive compared to Nissan?

A little. Shared mechanical parts (engine, transmission, suspension, brakes) cost roughly 10 to 25 percent more at an Infiniti counter than at a Nissan one, mostly because Infiniti dealers carry higher overhead. Infiniti-only parts such as leather upholstery, wood trim and exterior lighting are priced like Lexus parts through the dealer and substantially below BMW or Mercedes equivalents. For most maintenance items, Denso, Aisin, NGK or KYB parts cost 40 to 60 percent of dealer price. The [OEM glossary](/glossary/oem/) covers who makes what.

### Best year for used Infiniti Q50?

Buy a 2018-2022 with the 3.0L twin-turbo V6, in 300 hp Sport or 400 hp Red Sport 400 form. Those years are the best built and fastest, and they're still good value. A 2020 Q50 Red Sport 400 AWD with 60,000 km in Toronto or Vancouver lists around $32,000 to $38,000, far less than a comparable BMW M340i xDrive. The 2014-2015 cars with the naturally aspirated 3.7L V6 are also reliable but older. Be careful with the 2.0t trims, which used a Mercedes-Benz-sourced 2.0L turbo four, and skip one unless service history confirms regular oil changes.

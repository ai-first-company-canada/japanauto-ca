## Why buy a used Nissan in Canada

Nissan sits consistently in the top five of Canadian Japanese-brand sales and has more than 200 dealerships across the country. The Rogue is its biggest seller and usually makes Canada's top-10 SUV list. The case for a used Nissan in 2026 is price. The Rogue, Altima and Sentra lose value faster than their Toyota and Honda rivals, so a clean used Nissan from a Vancouver or Calgary lot often comes in $2,000 to $4,000 below a comparable RAV4 or CR-V at the same age and kilometres.

You aren't giving up much for that discount. The main models drive well, mid-trim cabin tech holds its own, and Nissan's [Intelligent AWD](/glossary/awd/) on the Rogue and Murano copes fine with Canadian winters. The real difference from buying a used Toyota is the 2013-2018 CVT. Ask about it first.

## Most popular used Nissan models

Rogues outnumber every other Nissan on lots in every Tier-1 city. A 2018 to 2022 Rogue SV with AWD and 60,000 to 110,000 km lists between $19,000 and $27,000 across Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary and the GTA suburbs, roughly $2,500 under an equivalent RAV4. The Pathfinder is Nissan's seven-seater, and the 2022 redesign won back some of the buyers the previous one lost. The Altima holds value a little better than the Sentra and makes a sensible used midsize sedan. The Sentra is the cheap commuter: low price, low running costs.

A few less common models deserve a look. The Frontier mid-size pickup, particularly the [PRO-4X](/glossary/awd/) trim, has become a Western Canada favourite for trades work where the Tacoma is over-budget. The Maxima held its own as a near-luxury sedan through 2023. The little Kicks sells better than you'd expect in Toronto and Montreal, mostly to first-time buyers with short commutes.

## Nissan AWD options for Canadian winters

Nissan's [Intelligent AWD](/glossary/awd/) is offered as standard or optional on Rogue, Murano, Pathfinder and the 2025-and-newer Kicks. The Frontier uses a separate part-time 4WD system. Like most systems in the class, Intelligent AWD is reactive: front-wheel drive until sensors detect slip, then the rear axle joins in. Subaru's always-on [Symmetrical AWD](/glossary/symmetrical-awd/) works differently. In Calgary or Edmonton a Rogue will pull away from a packed-snow stop without trouble, but on roads where grip changes every few metres the Subaru is better.

The Frontier PRO-4X is a proper truck. It has part-time 4WD with a transfer case, an electronic locking rear differential, Bilstein off-road shocks and skid plates, and it sells to oilfield and trades crews in Northern Alberta and rural BC.

## Nissan reliability and what to watch for

With a used Nissan, the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) is what you check first. From 2013 through 2018, the JATCO-built CVT in the Sentra, Altima, Rogue and Pathfinder had documented durability problems: the steel push-belt wore early, the software was poorly tuned, and the official fluid-change schedule was too long. Nissan responded by extending CVT warranty coverage on many affected cars. In most of Canada a class-action settlement stretched it to 7 years or 140,000 km on models such as the 2013-2016 Altima and 2013-2017 Sentra, and the Rogue got its own extension. Ask a Nissan dealer what applies to the specific VIN. On 2019 and newer cars, the CVT mostly behaves.

So in 2026, any 2013-2018 Nissan with a CVT needs a verified service history showing fluid changes at the recommended interval (typically 60,000 to 90,000 km), and ideally a CarFax Canada record showing no transmission-related warranty claims. Without those, walk. A replacement CVT costs $4,500 to $7,000 CAD installed, more than the depreciation discount saves you.

The 2.5L QR25 four-cylinder engine, the 3.5L VQ35 V6, and the 4.0L VQ40 in the Frontier all last 250,000+ km with standard maintenance. The engines are fine; the CVTs are the risk.

## Nissan parts and service in Canada

Nissan Canada's parts network has been around a long time, and dealer counters get OEM parts quickly. The CVT bulletins for the problem years are well documented. Ask a Nissan dealer to pull the warranty and service records by [VIN](/glossary/vin/) before you buy, and a reputable one will do it. ## Common questions

### Is Nissan reliable in Canada?

The engines are; some CVTs aren't. The 2.5L QR25, 3.5L VQ35 V6 and 4.0L VQ40 have a clean record in Transport Canada's defects database, with nothing major logged. It's a different story for [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) transmissions on 2013-2018 Sentra, Altima, Rogue, and Pathfinder, where Nissan extended CVT warranty coverage on many affected cars (in most of Canada, to 7 years or 140,000 km on models such as the 2013-2016 Altima and 2013-2017 Sentra) to address documented durability issues. For 2019 and newer Nissan CVTs the issues are largely resolved. For any 2013-2018 used Nissan, demand a verified service history before purchase.

### Are Nissan CVT transmissions a problem?

On the 2013-2018 Sentra, Altima, Rogue and Pathfinder, yes. The JATCO-built CVT had documented issues with belt wear, software calibration, and fluid maintenance schedules. Nissan extended CVT warranty coverage on many affected cars, and in most of Canada a class-action settlement set it at 7 years or 140,000 km for models such as the 2013-2016 Altima and 2013-2017 Sentra. By 2026 most of those extensions have run out. From 2019 the design is much better and the old problems rarely show up. On a used 2013-2018 Nissan, nothing matters more than proof of CVT fluid changes. Without it, assume you're buying a $5,000+ repair bill.

### Is a used Nissan Rogue a good buy in Canada?

A 2019 or newer, yes. A 2013-2018, only with paperwork. The current Rogue (2021+) drives well, its cabin tech keeps up with rivals, and the AWD handles Canadian winters. Pricing in 2026 typically runs $2,500 below the comparable Toyota RAV4. On 2013-2018 cars the [CVT](/glossary/cvt/) is the whole question, and you need verified service history. A clean 2020 Rogue SV AWD with 80,000 km in Calgary or Vancouver is good value.

### Which Nissan models offer AWD?

[AWD](/glossary/awd/) is standard or optional on Rogue, Murano, Pathfinder and the 2025-and-newer Kicks, and the Frontier mid-size pickup has 4WD. The Sentra and Altima are FWD only in the Canadian market. On the crossovers, Intelligent AWD is reactive and only drives the rear axle once a wheel slips. The Frontier has a part-time 4WD system with a transfer case, built for real off-road use. The PRO-4X Frontier trim adds an electronic locking rear differential and Bilstein off-road shocks.

### Best year for used Nissan Altima?

Go for 2019-2023, ideally an SR or Platinum. That generation is better built and more durable, and it holds its value better. Pricing in 2026 for a 2020 Altima SR with 80,000 to 110,000 km typically runs $19,000 to $24,000. The 2.0L VC-Turbo variable-compression engine introduced in 2019 is clever but complicated. Buy the 2.5L base engine. Skip the 2013-2018 generation unless the service history is complete.

### Do Nissan Rogues hold their value in Canada?

Less well than RAV4, slightly better than Equinox, and roughly even with CR-V at three years from new. Three-year retention on a Rogue SV typically lands around 58 to 62 percent of original MSRP across Canadian Black Book averages, against roughly 70 percent for the RAV4. That gap is the whole reason to buy a used Rogue. It does roughly what a RAV4 does for noticeably less money. Remember that it will lose value faster for you too when you sell.

### Is Nissan or Toyota better for Canadian roads?

Closer than the ads suggest. Toyota's powertrains have the better record, especially its hybrids and transmissions. Nissan is cheaper to buy used, and the Rogue and Murano AWD is better calibrated than you might expect. With winter tires, AWD versions of both do the same job in snow. Toyota is the safer buy if you'll sell in a few years; Nissan is the better deal if you'll drive it into the ground and the CVT checks out.
